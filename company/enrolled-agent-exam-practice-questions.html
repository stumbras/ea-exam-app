Free Enrolled Agent Exam Practice Questions (IRS SEE, all 3 parts) with Answers

The IRS Special Enrollment Examination (SEE) has three parts: Individuals, Businesses, and Representation, Practices and Procedures. Each part has 100 multiple-choice questions and lasts 3.5 hours. The current window runs July 1, 2026 to February 28, 2027 and tests tax year 2025 law. The cost is $317 per part, and PSI gives the exam. There is no education or experience requirement.

Part 1 · Individuals

1. Fay is 19, a full-time student who lives with her parents all year, and she meets all the tests to be their qualifying child. She earned $8,000 at a part-time job. Can Fay claim the earned income credit on her own return?

  1. No. Students can never claim it.
  2. Yes, if her parents choose not to claim her at all.
  3. No. She is a qualifying child of another person.
  4. Yes. Her wages are low enough.
Show answer

C. No. She is a qualifying child of another person.

A person who is the qualifying child of another taxpayer cannot claim the EIC, even if the other taxpayer does not actually claim her. The rule looks at whether she meets the tests, not at who files.

2. Box 12 of Elena's Form W-2 shows $14,200 with code DD. What effect does this amount have on her return?

  1. None; it is for information only.
  2. It is an adjustment on Schedule 1.
  3. It is deducted on Schedule A.
  4. It is added to her taxable wages.
Show answer

A. None; it is for information only.

W-2 box 12 codes: DD is the cost of employer health coverage, shown for information only and not taxable. Other codes: C is taxable group-term life over $50,000, and W is HSA contributions through the employer.

3. Earl lends his son $8,000 with no interest so that the son can buy a used car for getting to work. Must Earl report imputed interest income or a gift of the forgone interest?

  1. Yes, but only the gift, not the income.
  2. No. Family loans are always exempt.
  3. Yes, at the applicable federal rate.
  4. No. The loan is $10,000 or less.
Show answer

D. No. The loan is $10,000 or less.

Below-market gift loans: forgone interest is a gift by the lender and interest income to the lender. De minimis rule: no imputation for loans of $10,000 or less, unless the money is used to buy income-producing assets.

4. A worker is paid time-and-a-half for overtime required by federal law. What part can be deducted under the 2025 overtime deduction?

  1. Nothing unless the taxpayer itemizes.
  2. Only the extra half of pay above the regular rate, up to $12,500 ($25,000 on a joint return).
  3. All pay for overtime hours, up to $12,500.
  4. All pay for overtime hours, including the regular rate, with no limit.
Show answer

B. Only the extra half of pay above the regular rate, up to $12,500 ($25,000 on a joint return).

The deduction covers the premium portion of overtime required by the Fair Labor Standards Act. For time-and-a-half pay, that is the half. It is claimed on Schedule 1-A and phases out above $150,000 of modified AGI ($300,000 joint).

5. In 2025 Yan has a $5,000 short-term capital gain and a $12,000 long-term capital loss, and no other sales. What is the result on his return?

  1. A $3,000 deduction; $4,000 long-term carryover.
  2. A $7,000 deduction against ordinary income.
  3. A $5,000 gain taxed; $12,000 carried over.
  4. A $3,000 deduction; $4,000 short-term carryover.
Show answer

A. A $3,000 deduction; $4,000 long-term carryover.

Capital gains and losses are netted: the $12,000 long-term loss absorbs the $5,000 short-term gain, leaving a $7,000 net long-term loss. $3,000 is deducted this year and $4,000 carries forward as a long-term loss.

6. In May, Oona gets a form from her IRA trustee showing her 2025 contributions, a rollover she made and the account's value at year end. Which form is it, and does she attach it to her return?

  1. Form 5498; she keeps it for her records.
  2. Form 1099-R; she attaches it to the return.
  3. Form 8606; the trustee files it for her.
  4. Form 5498; she attaches it to the return.
Show answer

A. Form 5498; she keeps it for her records.

Form 5498 is IRA Contribution Information: contributions, rollovers, conversions and year-end value, sent by the IRA trustee by May 31. It is for information; the taxpayer does not file it.

7. Which statement about contributions to a health savings account is correct?

  1. Contributions must stop at age 59½.
  2. Contributions can be made in stock or other property.
  3. Contributions must be made in cash, and there is no income limit on who can contribute.
  4. Contributions are not allowed for taxpayers with income over $220,000 on a joint return.
Show answer

C. Contributions must be made in cash, and there is no income limit on who can contribute.

An eligible person can contribute cash up to the yearly limit whatever their income. An HSA has no required distributions. Contributions stop once the person enrolls in Medicare.

8. Uwe gave his niece $25,000 in cash in 2025. It was his only gift of the year and the first taxable gift of his life. What must he do?

  1. Nothing; gifts to family are exempt.
  2. File Form 709; no gift tax is paid.
  3. File Form 709 and pay tax on $6,000.
  4. Report $6,000 on his Form 1040.
Show answer

B. File Form 709; no gift tax is paid.

A gift of more than the $19,000 annual exclusion to one person requires Form 709. The $6,000 taxable gift uses part of his lifetime exclusion through the unified credit, so no gift tax is actually paid. The niece has no income.

9. A taxpayer died on September 10, 2025. Her final Form 1040 is due:

  1. April 15, 2026
  2. December 10, 2025
  3. June 10, 2026
  4. 9 months after death
Show answer

A. April 15, 2026

The final income tax return is due on the regular due date. The 9-month rule is for Form 706.

10. A paid preparer completes a return that claims the earned income credit. Which form must the preparer complete to show due diligence?

  1. Form 8867
  2. Form 8332
  3. Form 8862
  4. Form 8857
Show answer

A. Form 8867

Form 8867, Paid Preparer's Due Diligence Checklist, is required for returns claiming the EIC, the child tax credit, the American opportunity credit or head of household status. Form 8862 is the taxpayer's form after a credit was denied.

11. A taxpayer owes AMT for 2025. Where does it reach Form 1040?

  1. Form 8801, then Schedule 1, then Form 1040 line 8
  2. Form 6251, then Schedule 2, then Form 1040 line 17
  3. Form 6251, then Schedule 3, then Form 1040 line 20
  4. Form 6251, then directly on Form 1040 line 16
Show answer

B. Form 6251, then Schedule 2, then Form 1040 line 17

AMT is figured on Form 6251 and carried to Schedule 2, Part I, which goes to Form 1040, line 17. It is added to the regular tax before credits.

12. A fire destroys a rental building, and the insurance payment is more than the building's basis. How can the owner postpone the gain?

  1. By buying similar replacement property within the replacement period, generally 2 years after the end of the year the gain was realized.
  2. By depositing the insurance money in a savings account and buying any kind of property within 6 months of the fire.
  3. By giving the insurance money to a family member.
  4. The gain cannot be postponed.
Show answer

A. By buying similar replacement property within the replacement period, generally 2 years after the end of the year the gain was realized.

This is an involuntary conversion. Gain is postponed to the extent the proceeds are reinvested in property similar in use. The period is 3 years for condemned business or investment real estate, and 4 years for a main home in a federally declared disaster.

Part 2 · Businesses

13. A paycheck issued after death for work the decedent performed before death is:

  1. Income in respect of a decedent
  2. Non-taxable
  3. Excess compensation to the estate
  4. Deferred income
Show answer

A. Income in respect of a decedent

Income the person earned but hadn't received before death is income in respect of a decedent (IRD). Whoever receives it reports it, and it keeps the same character.

14. A corporation is just starting up. How may it treat its organizational costs?

  1. It must capitalize them with no deduction until the corporation is dissolved.
  2. It can deduct all of them in the first year, whatever the amount.
  3. It amortizes all of them over 60 months.
  4. It can deduct up to $5,000 in the first year and amortize the rest over 180 months.
Show answer

D. It can deduct up to $5,000 in the first year and amortize the rest over 180 months.

The $5,000 is reduced dollar for dollar when the costs are more than $50,000. Organizational costs include legal fees for the charter and state incorporation fees. Costs of issuing or selling stock do not qualify.

15. A former C corporation elected S status in 2023. In 2025 it sells an asset that had built-in gain on the day the election took effect. What applies?

  1. The built-in gains tax, at 21%, because the sale is within 5 years of the election.
  2. No corporate tax at all, because S corporations never pay any tax of their own.
  3. The personal holding company tax.
  4. The accumulated earnings tax.
Show answer

A. The built-in gains tax, at 21%, because the sale is within 5 years of the election.

A C corporation that becomes an S corporation pays corporate tax on built-in gains recognized during the 5-year recognition period. The gain still passes through to shareholders, reduced by the tax paid.

16. Land: sale price $100,000, basis $40,000, no liabilities. $20,000 received in 2025. Gain recognized in 2025:

  1. $8,000
  2. $60,000
  3. $12,000
  4. $20,000
Show answer

C. $12,000

Gross profit % = 60,000 / 100,000 = 60% × 20,000 = 12,000.

17. An S corporation that was a C corporation until 2023 sells land in 2025 that had a built-in gain on the conversion date. What tax may apply at the corporate level?

  1. The personal holding company tax at 20% on the whole gain from the land sale
  2. The built-in gains tax at 21%, since it's within the 5-year recognition period
  3. None; an S corporation never pays tax at the entity level on any gain
  4. The accumulated earnings tax, because the gain was held inside the company
Show answer

B. The built-in gains tax at 21%, since it's within the 5-year recognition period

§1374 taxes net recognized built-in gain at the highest corporate rate (21%) when an S corporation that converted from a C corporation sells appreciated assets within 5 years of the conversion. The gain also passes through to shareholders, reduced by the tax.

18. What is the main tax trade-off of an LLC owner electing S corporation status?

  1. Profits are taxed twice, once to the company and again to the owner
  2. Owner draws replace payroll, so no W-2 or Form 941 is ever needed
  3. SE tax only on salary, but salary must be reasonable and run through payroll
  4. No income tax at all on profits, because the S corporation pays a flat 21%
Show answer

C. SE tax only on salary, but salary must be reasonable and run through payroll

Distributions above a reasonable salary are not subject to employment taxes. Payroll, Form 1120-S and shareholder basis tracking add cost and work.

19. A business wants to change from the cash method to the accrual method. It files…

  1. Form 3115
  2. Form 8832
  3. Form 1128
  4. Form 4562
Show answer

A. Form 3115

Form 3115 = change in accounting METHOD. Form 1128 = change in tax YEAR.

20. Quill Co. sponsors a 401(k) plan covering 40 employees and needs more time to file the plan's annual return. Which forms are the annual return and its extension?

  1. Form 5330; extended with Form 4868.
  2. Form 5500; extended with Form 5558.
  3. Form 5500-EZ; extended with Form 7004.
  4. Form 5498; extended with Form 8809.
Show answer

B. Form 5500; extended with Form 5558.

Form 5500 is the Annual Return/Report of Employee Benefit Plan, due the last day of the seventh month after the plan year ends. Form 5558 extends the time to file it.

21. A new business has $53,000 of start-up costs in 2025. How much can it deduct in 2025, before amortization of the rest?

  1. $5,000
  2. $0
  3. $53,000
  4. $2,000
Show answer

D. $2,000

The §195 deduction is $5,000, reduced dollar for dollar by start-up costs over $50,000: $5,000 − $3,000 = $2,000. The remaining $51,000 is amortized over 180 months.

22. A trust has distributable net income of $20,000 for 2025, and the trustee, as the trust allows, pays the beneficiary $30,000. What is the trust's distribution deduction, and how much is taxable to the beneficiary?

  1. $20,000 deduction; $30,000 taxable.
  2. $30,000 for each.
  3. $30,000 deduction; $20,000 taxable.
  4. $20,000 for each.
Show answer

D. $20,000 for each.

DNI (distributable net income) caps what a trust or estate can deduct for distributions and what the beneficiaries are taxed on. The extra $10,000 is a tax-free distribution of principal.

23. A large corporation (taxable income of $1 million or more in a recent year) wants to avoid an estimated tax penalty. Which safe harbor can it use?

  1. 90% of the current-year tax, the same as an individual taxpayer
  2. 110% of last year's tax for all four payments of the year
  3. 100% of the current-year tax; last year's tax only for the first payment
  4. 100% of last year's tax for all four payments, like a small company
Show answer

C. 100% of the current-year tax; last year's tax only for the first payment

Large corporations can base only the first installment on the prior year's tax and must make up any shortfall in the second installment.

24. For property placed in service in a tax year beginning in 2025, what is the section 179 deduction limit?

  1. $2,500,000, reduced above $4,000,000 of property
  2. No dollar limit, because bonus depreciation is 100%
  3. $1,000,000, reduced above $2,500,000 of property
  4. $1,250,000, reduced above $3,130,000 of property
Show answer

A. $2,500,000, reduced above $4,000,000 of property

The 2025 tax law raised the section 179 limit to $2.5 million with a $4 million phase-out threshold. Section 179 is also limited to the business's taxable income from active trades or businesses.

Part 3 · Representation

25. A specified tax return preparer's client insists on filing on paper. The preparer should:

  1. Refuse the engagement and tell the client to find a preparer who files on paper
  2. E-file anyway
  3. File Form 8453
  4. Obtain the client's signed statement and attach Form 8948 to the paper return
Show answer

D. Obtain the client's signed statement and attach Form 8948 to the paper return

Client choice is an exception to the e-file mandate.

26. Before contacting a taxpayer's customers about her tax liability, the IRS must generally:

  1. Notify the Taxpayer Advocate
  2. Get the taxpayer's written consent before each contact
  3. Obtain a court order
  4. Give the taxpayer advance notice at least 45 days before the contact
Show answer

D. Give the taxpayer advance notice at least 45 days before the contact

§7602(c) requires the IRS to give the taxpayer reasonable advance notice (at least 45 days) before contacting third parties about the taxpayer's liability.

27. A former IRS revenue agent leaves the IRS and joins a private practice. Under §10.25, what may he NOT do?

  1. Prepare any tax return for pay during his first year in private practice after leaving
  2. Represent any taxpayer before the IRS for 10 years after he leaves
  3. Advertise that he once worked for the IRS, even in truthful terms
  4. Represent anyone in a matter he personally and substantially worked on while at the IRS
Show answer

D. Represent anyone in a matter he personally and substantially worked on while at the IRS

§10.25 bars former government employees from representing others in particular matters they participated in personally and substantially, with other limits for certain periods.

28. How long does a taxpayer have to claim a refund based on a bad debt or worthless security?

  1. 2 years from the date of the loss
  2. 10 years from the date of loss
  3. 7 years from the return's due date
  4. 3 years from the return's due date
Show answer

C. 7 years from the return's due date

§6511(d)(1) gives a longer period for these two items.

29. Quon, an EA, no longer represents a client and wants his name taken off the client's power of attorney on file with the IRS. The client will not cooperate in revoking it. What can Quon do by himself?

  1. Wait for the form to lapse by itself after one year.
  2. Nothing; only the client can end it.
  3. File a new Form 2848 naming no one.
  4. Send the IRS a signed copy marked WITHDRAW.
Show answer

D. Send the IRS a signed copy marked WITHDRAW.

A representative withdraws by writing WITHDRAW across the top of the first page of the Form 2848, signing and dating below it, and sending it to the IRS (or by a signed statement if he has no copy). A taxpayer ends a power of attorney the same way with the word REVOKE.

30. Which statement about a closing agreement under §7121 is correct?

  1. It is final and conclusive on the matters covered, reopened only for fraud, malfeasance or misrepresentation of a material fact
  2. It is provisional, and either the taxpayer or the IRS may reopen it at any time within 3 years of signing it
  3. It is another name for an installment agreement to pay a balance due
  4. It wipes out all of the taxpayer's future tax liabilities for every year
Show answer

A. It is final and conclusive on the matters covered, reopened only for fraud, malfeasance or misrepresentation of a material fact

Closing agreements (Forms 866 and 906) are binding on both the taxpayer and the IRS.

31. Fay mails her paper return at the post office on April 15, 2026 and gets a postmark for that date. Her neighbor sends his the same day by a private carrier's ordinary ground service, which is not on the IRS list of designated services. Both returns reach the IRS on April 21. Whose return is on time?

  1. Neither return.
  2. Both returns.
  3. Fay's only.
  4. The neighbor's only.
Show answer

C. Fay's only.

A return is treated as filed on the date of the U.S. Postal Service postmark if it is mailed on or before the due date. The same rule applies to a private delivery service only when the specific service is one the IRS has designated. Otherwise the return is filed on the day it is received.

32. A business changes its responsible party. Which form reports this, and when?

  1. Form 8822-B, within 60 days
  2. Form 8822, within 30 days
  3. Form SS-4, within 90 days
  4. Form 8832, within 75 days
Show answer

A. Form 8822-B, within 60 days

Form 8822-B covers business mailing or location changes and responsible party changes.

33. Which action is generally prohibited when dealing with an IRS employee?

  1. Asking for a supervisor conference
  2. Providing a signed power of attorney
  3. Offering a gift intended to influence the employee's official actions
  4. Requesting a written explanation of an adjustment from the employee
Show answer

C. Offering a gift intended to influence the employee's official actions

Circular 230 §10.51 and applicable federal ethics rules prohibit improper conduct involving government employees.

34. Quin receives a notice of deficiency for $18,000 of tax and penalties for one year. He wants the simplest and cheapest route in Tax Court. What is true of the small tax case procedure?

  1. It is decided by a jury.
  2. It covers up to $10,000 a year, with an appeal.
  3. It covers up to $50,000 a year, with no appeal.
  4. It needs payment of the tax first.
Show answer

C. It covers up to $50,000 a year, with no appeal.

The Tax Court's small tax case (S case) procedure is available when the amount in dispute, including penalties, is $50,000 or less for each year. The procedure is informal, and the decision cannot be appealed by either side and is not precedent.

35. The IRS requests documents from an EA about a client matter. The EA does not have them but knows the client's former accountant does. The EA must:

  1. Withdraw from the engagement
  2. Tell the IRS who she believes has the documents
  3. Do nothing, because she doesn't possess them
  4. Obtain the documents from the accountant
Show answer

B. Tell the IRS who she believes has the documents

§10.20: provide information about who has the records; reasonable inquiry of the client, no duty to ask third parties.

36. Rex owes $60,000 and the revenue officer wants to seize and sell the house he lives in. What is required before the IRS can seize a taxpayer's main home?

  1. Only the approval of the group manager.
  2. Nothing beyond the final notice of intent.
  3. The consent of the taxpayer's mortgage lender.
  4. Written approval from a federal district court.
Show answer

D. Written approval from a federal district court.

Seizure of a principal residence: the IRS needs written approval from a U.S. district court judge or magistrate, and it cannot seize a main home at all for a liability of $5,000 or less. Business assets of an individual (tangible property or real property used in the trade or business) need the written approval of an IRS area director, unless collection is in jeopardy.
