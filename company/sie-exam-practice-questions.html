Free SIE Exam Practice Questions with Answers (FINRA Securities Industry Essentials)

The Securities Industry Essentials (SIE) exam has 75 scored questions in 1 hour 45 minutes. The passing score is 70 and the fee is $100. Anyone 18 or older can take it with no firm sponsor, and a pass is good for four years.

Below are 30 free practice questions. Pick your answer, then tap Show answer.

1. Rule 144A allows restricted securities to be resold to

  1. Retail customers in the issuer's state
  2. Employees of the issuer only
  3. Any accredited investor
  4. Qualified institutional buyers
Show answer

D. Qualified institutional buyers

Rule 144A creates a market for private placements among qualified institutional buyers (generally institutions with at least $100 million in securities).

2. The Federal Reserve sells Treasury securities in the open market. The most likely effect is

  1. Lower bond yields
  2. More money in the banking system and lower interest rates
  3. Less money in the banking system and higher interest rates
  4. No effect on interest rates
Show answer

C. Less money in the banking system and higher interest rates

Selling securities drains reserves from banks, which tightens money and pushes rates up.

3. An offering sold only to residents of the issuer's home state can be exempt under

  1. The resale exemption for control stock (Rule 144)
  2. The intrastate exemption (Rule 147)
  3. The private placement exemption (Regulation D)
  4. The small offering exemption (Regulation A)
Show answer

B. The intrastate exemption (Rule 147)

Intrastate offerings are registered with the state instead of the SEC.

4. During the cooling-off period a registered rep may

  1. promise the client shares at a set price
  2. send a final prospectus along with each customer confirmation
  3. accept orders and payment
  4. take indications of interest and send a preliminary prospectus
Show answer

D. take indications of interest and send a preliminary prospectus

No sales or binding orders are allowed until the registration is effective.

5. In a best efforts underwriting, shares the underwriter cannot sell

  1. Are split among the selling group members to hold
  2. Stay with the issuer; the underwriter does not buy them
  3. Are sold to the Federal Reserve at a discount
  4. Must be bought by the underwriter at the offering price
Show answer

B. Stay with the issuer; the underwriter does not buy them

Best efforts means the underwriter acts as an agent. It sells what it can and is not on the hook for the rest.

6. The preliminary prospectus (red herring) does NOT include

  1. The final offering price
  2. The use of the money raised
  3. The risks of the investment
  4. A description of the business
Show answer

A. The final offering price

The red herring is used during the cooling-off period. The final price and effective date are added in the final prospectus.

7. Of the underwriting spread, the largest part usually goes to

  1. The manager's fee
  2. The selling concession
  3. The underwriting fee
  4. The SEC filing fee
Show answer

B. The selling concession

The spread is split into the manager's fee, the underwriting fee and the selling concession. The selling concession is the biggest share.

8. A tombstone advertisement for a new issue

  1. Must include the full text of the prospectus for readers
  2. Replaces the need to deliver a prospectus to buyers
  3. Gives basic facts and says it is not an offer to sell
  4. Can promise investors a minimum return on the issue
Show answer

C. Gives basic facts and says it is not an offer to sell

A tombstone lists the issuer, the security, the amount and where to get a prospectus. It is not an offer.

9. A general obligation municipal bond is backed by

  1. The issuer's full taxing power
  2. The U.S. Treasury
  3. A bank letter of credit only
  4. Revenue from one facility
Show answer

A. The issuer's full taxing power

GO bonds are backed by taxes; revenue bonds are paid from the project's income.

10. U.S. savings bonds (Series EE and I) are

  1. Traded every day on the New York Stock Exchange
  2. Non-marketable: they cannot be sold to other investors
  3. Backed by Fannie Mae rather than by the U.S. Treasury
  4. Issued only in amounts of $1 million or more
Show answer

B. Non-marketable: they cannot be sold to other investors

Savings bonds are registered to the owner and cashed in with the Treasury, not traded.

11. A corporation offers current shareholders the right to buy new shares at a discount for a few weeks. These are

  1. convertible bonds
  2. warrants
  3. rights
  4. call options
Show answer

C. rights

Rights are short-term and protect existing holders' proportionate ownership.

12. A customer will invest $100,000 in one fund family for the long term. Which share class usually costs least?

  1. Class C, because it has no front-end load
  2. Class A, because of breakpoints
  3. Class B
  4. Class C
Show answer

B. Class A, because of breakpoints

Big long-term purchases get lower front-end loads at breakpoints and lower yearly fees.

13. Which mutual fund share class is usually best for a short holding period?

  1. Class A
  2. None, all are the same
  3. Class C
  4. Class B
Show answer

C. Class C

C shares have a level load and a small CDSC that typically ends after one year.

14. A bond with a 6% coupon trades at 104. Its yield to maturity is

  1. Exactly 6%
  2. More than 6%
  3. Less than 6%
  4. Zero
Show answer

C. Less than 6%

Premium bonds have yields below the coupon.

15. For a premium callable bond, the lowest yield is usually the

  1. Nominal yield
  2. Yield to call
  3. Current yield
  4. Yield to maturity
Show answer

B. Yield to call

For premium bonds: nominal > current > yield to maturity > yield to call.

16. A company calls its high-coupon bonds after rates fall. The bondholders face

  1. Call risk and lower yields for reinvesting
  2. More credit risk from the issuer going forward
  3. Currency risk on the final principal payment
  4. Higher income taxes on the call price paid
Show answer

A. Call risk and lower yields for reinvesting

When bonds are called, investors get their money back early and must reinvest at the new lower rates.

17. A firm must keep customer identification (CIP) records for

  1. Five years after the account closes
  2. Ten years after the first trade
  3. One year after the account opens
  4. Only while the account is open
Show answer

A. Five years after the account closes

CIP identity records are kept for five years after the account is closed.

18. Which stage of money laundering hides the source by moving money through many transactions?

  1. Structuring
  2. Placement
  3. Integration
  4. Layering
Show answer

D. Layering

Placement puts cash in, layering hides the trail, integration brings it back as clean money.

19. Promising a customer they won't lose money on a trade is

  1. allowed if the firm agrees to pay any loss
  2. allowed for bonds
  3. allowed with written consent
  4. prohibited (guaranteeing against loss)
Show answer

D. prohibited (guaranteeing against loss)

Reps may not guarantee customers against loss.

20. A customer is short a stock at $50 and wants to cap the loss. Which order?

  1. A sell limit above $50
  2. A buy stop above $50
  3. A buy limit below $50
  4. A sell stop below $50
Show answer

B. A buy stop above $50

Buy stops sit above the market and protect a short position.

21. A Suspicious Activity Report is generally required for suspicious transactions of at least

  1. $100,000
  2. $10,000
  3. $5,000
  4. $1,000
Show answer

C. $5,000

Broker-dealers file SARs for suspicious activity of $5,000 or more; CTRs are for cash over $10,000.

22. Under suitability and Reg BI, quantitative suitability looks at

  1. How much profit the firm earns on the account each year
  2. Whether one trade fits the customer's goals
  3. Whether a series of trades is excessive taken together
  4. Only the customer's tax bracket and income
Show answer

C. Whether a series of trades is excessive taken together

It is aimed at churning: too many trades even if each one alone seems fine.

23. To open an account for a corporation, the firm needs a

  1. Corporate resolution naming who may trade
  2. A letter of intent signed by the CEO
  3. A copy of the company's latest prospectus
  4. A Form U4 for each corporate officer
Show answer

A. Corporate resolution naming who may trade

The resolution shows who is authorized to act for the company; margin also needs the charter or bylaws.

24. Securities in a UTMA account belong to

  1. The minor
  2. The custodian
  3. The parent who gave them
  4. The broker-dealer
Show answer

A. The minor

Gifts to a UTMA are irrevocable and owned by the minor; the account uses the minor's Social Security number.

25. SIPC covers each separate customer capacity up to

  1. $250,000, including $100,000 in cash
  2. $500,000, including $250,000 in cash
  3. market losses up to $500,000
  4. $1,000,000 of securities
Show answer

B. $500,000, including $250,000 in cash

SIPC replaces missing assets when a firm fails; it never covers market losses.

26. The most severe FINRA penalty for an individual is

  1. a bar from the industry
  2. a censure
  3. a fine
  4. a 30-day suspension
Show answer

A. a bar from the industry

A bar stops someone from associating with any member firm.

27. The firm element of continuing education

  1. Is training each firm provides on its own products and rules
  2. Is a course taken once every 10 years by every rep
  3. Is run by the SEC in the same way for all firms
  4. Is required only for principals and not for reps
Show answer

A. Is training each firm provides on its own products and rules

The regulatory element is FINRA's annual course; the firm element is each firm's own yearly training plan.

28. A candidate fails the SIE twice and retakes in February 2027. How long must she wait after the second failure?

  1. 60 days
  2. 30 days
  3. 180 days
  4. 15 days
Show answer

D. 15 days

From January 19, 2027 the wait after a first or second failure drops to 15 days.

29. A rep leaves the industry. Under FINRA's Maintaining Qualifications Program she can keep her exams valid for up to

  1. 5 years, by doing annual continuing education
  2. 2 years, with no conditions of any kind
  3. 10 years, by paying a yearly FINRA fee
  4. Her whole life, once she has passed them
Show answer

A. 5 years, by doing annual continuing education

Normally qualifications lapse after 2 years out of the industry. The program extends that to 5 years if she completes yearly CE.

30. A rep's Form U4 must be updated for a reportable event, such as a criminal charge, within

  1. 1 year
  2. 90 days
  3. 5 business days
  4. 30 days
Show answer

D. 30 days

Amendments are generally due within 30 days.
