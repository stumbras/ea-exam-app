Free EDPNA Exam Practice Questions with Answers (Social Security Representative)

EDPNA stands for Eligible for Direct Payment Non-Attorney. Passing SSA's exam lets a non-attorney representative be paid fees directly from a claimant's past-due Social Security benefits. The exam has 50 multiple-choice questions and the pass mark is 70%.

Below are 24 free practice questions. Pick your answer, then tap Show answer.

1. What is the minimum passing percentage for the EDPNA exam?

  1. 90%
  2. 70%
  3. 50%
  4. 60%
Show answer

B. 70%

SSA states a 70 percent passing threshold. Source: SSA EDPNA webpage.

2. An SSDI beneficiary's spouse is 45 and caring for their 10-year-old child. Which family members may receive auxiliary benefits?

  1. Only the child
  2. Only the spouse
  3. Neither of them, until the spouse reaches age 62
  4. The spouse (caring for a child under 16) and the child
Show answer

D. The spouse (caring for a child under 16) and the child

Both are eligible, subject to the family maximum.

3. Benefits ended 3 years ago because of work. The same condition now stops her from working. What is the fastest route?

  1. A brand-new application, and wait for the state DDS decision
  2. Ask the Appeals Council to reopen the old termination decision
  3. Expedited reinstatement, with up to 6 months of provisional pay
  4. Nothing can be done; she must wait 5 more years before applying
Show answer

C. Expedited reinstatement, with up to 6 months of provisional pay

Expedited reinstatement is available within 5 years of the termination.

4. What is the maximum monthly Social Security benefit for a worker retiring at full retirement age in 2026?

  1. $2,830
  2. $3,822
  3. $4,152
  4. $5,108
Show answer

C. $4,152

For SSI the 2026 maximum federal payment is $994 (individual) or $1,491 (couple). $2,830 is the 2026 blind SGA amount.

5. A claimant's RFC is light work. Her past relevant work was cashier (light, as generally performed). At step 4 she is

  1. not disabled, because she can return to past relevant work as generally performed
  2. disabled, because she can no longer do medium or heavy work of any kind
  3. sent to the grid rules first
  4. disabled if she is over 50
Show answer

A. not disabled, because she can return to past relevant work as generally performed

If the claimant can do PRW as actually performed or as generally performed, the claim ends at step 4.

6. A claimant's only past job was a composite of warehouse worker and truck driver. At step 4 the ALJ should

  1. judge it only as the claimant actually performed it
  2. judge it as generally performed for each part
  3. count each part as a separate past job
  4. skip step 4
Show answer

A. judge it only as the claimant actually performed it

Composite jobs have no 'generally performed' counterpart.

7. At step 2 of the adult disability evaluation, SSA primarily addresses what?

  1. Whether a medically determinable severe impairment meets duration rules
  2. Whether an appeal is late
  3. Whether the fee is approved
  4. Whether there is an attorney
Show answer

A. Whether a medically determinable severe impairment meets duration rules

Step 2 considers severity and the statutory duration requirement. Source: 20 CFR 404.1520(c); 416.920(c).

8. A claimant has a back disorder that does not meet listing 1.15 but she also has obesity. SSA must

  1. decide only on the back disorder
  2. find her disabled automatically
  3. consider the combined effect of all impairments, including obesity (SSR 19-2p)
  4. ignore obesity, because it is not a listed impairment in its own right
Show answer

C. consider the combined effect of all impairments, including obesity (SSR 19-2p)

Impairments are considered in combination at every step.

9. Fibromyalgia can be a medically determinable impairment under

  1. only the Listings for joint disorders, when an X-ray shows joint damage
  2. no ruling; it never qualifies
  3. the claimant's own statement alone
  4. SSR 12-2p, when an acceptable medical source makes the diagnosis using the listed criteria
Show answer

D. SSR 12-2p, when an acceptable medical source makes the diagnosis using the listed criteria

SSR 12-2p sets the criteria SSA uses.

10. Why are detailed medical source statements potentially important?

  1. They replace the hearing decision
  2. They may document specific functional limitations
  3. They guarantee fee approval
  4. They automatically establish insured status
Show answer

B. They may document specific functional limitations

Functional evidence supports RFC assessment, though conclusions are reserved to SSA. Source: 20 CFR 404.1520b; 404.1545.

11. A claimant reports severe headaches but no objective tests confirm a cause. Under SSR 19-4p, a primary headache disorder can be established by

  1. a friend's letter
  2. the claimant's own statement alone, if it is detailed and consistent with her treatment history and her daily headache diary
  3. an MRI showing headaches
  4. a detailed medical description of headache events from an acceptable medical source, with exclusion of other causes
Show answer

D. a detailed medical description of headache events from an acceptable medical source, with exclusion of other causes

Imaging is usually normal; the record from an acceptable source matters.

12. A claimant's treating psychologist says she has 'marked' limits but gives no explanation and her own notes show normal mental status exams. Under 20 CFR 404.1520c the opinion is likely

  1. inadmissible
  2. binding on the ALJ because of the treating relationship
  3. less persuasive because of weak supportability and consistency
  4. controlling
Show answer

C. less persuasive because of weak supportability and consistency

Unsupported, inconsistent opinions get less weight.

13. The Appeals Council may review an ALJ decision on its own motion within

  1. 30 days
  2. one year of the decision
  3. 4 years
  4. 60 days of the decision
Show answer

D. 60 days of the decision

Own-motion review can occur even if the claimant does not appeal.

14. A federal judge finds the ALJ misapplied the law and sends the case back for a new decision. This is

  1. a dismissal
  2. a sentence four remand
  3. a sentence six remand
  4. a reopening
Show answer

B. a sentence four remand

Sentence six remands are for new evidence without a ruling.

15. A representative wants to send written questions to the medical expert after the hearing. How?

  1. Propose them to the ALJ, who approves and sends them
  2. Email them straight to the medical expert's office
  3. Mail them to the Appeals Council and ask it to forward them
  4. Call the medical expert and ask the questions by phone
Show answer

A. Propose them to the ALJ, who approves and sends them

The ME answers only to the ALJ. The claimant and representative get copies and may rebut or request a supplemental hearing.

16. After the Appeals Council denies review, the claimant may

  1. Ask the ALJ to hold a second hearing
  2. File a civil action in federal district court within 60 days
  3. Ask the field office to reopen the case for a new hearing
  4. Appeal to the state supreme court
Show answer

B. File a civil action in federal district court within 60 days

The Appeals Council decision or denial is SSA's final decision; the next step is federal court.

17. If an EDPNA’s required professional liability coverage lapses without an approved inactive status, what is a likely consequence?

  1. Automatic state bar admission
  2. No consequence under any circumstances
  3. Automatic approval of future fees
  4. Loss of direct-pay eligibility under SSA rules
Show answer

D. Loss of direct-pay eligibility under SSA rules

Continuous insurance is required for direct payment and lapses can trigger ineligibility. Source: 20 CFR 404.1717; SSA EDPNA webpage.

18. A claimant has no past-due benefits after a favorable decision. Under the fee agreement, the representative

  1. Collects 25% of future monthly benefits instead
  2. Receives the full $9,200 cap anyway
  3. Bills the claimant directly for $9,200
  4. Receives no fee under the fee agreement process
Show answer

D. Receives no fee under the fee agreement process

The fee agreement fee is based on past-due benefits; with none, the fee is zero.

19. A representative may charge the claimant for out-of-pocket costs, such as fees to copy medical records,

  1. only with Appeals Council approval in advance, because expenses are treated exactly like fees for SSA services
  2. never
  3. without fee authorization, because expenses are not fees, but SSA does not pay them from withheld past-due benefits
  4. only if the case is lost
Show answer

C. without fee authorization, because expenses are not fees, but SSA does not pay them from withheld past-due benefits

Expenses are billed to the claimant directly.

20. An ALJ authorizes a $2,000 fee. SSA pays it directly. Six months later SSA pays another $2,000 for the same claim by mistake, and a year later asks for a refund within 30 days. What should the representative do?

  1. Keep it because the error was SSA's
  2. Keep it as payment for later work
  3. Refund it to the claimant instead
  4. Refund the $2,000 overpayment to SSA within 30 days
Show answer

D. Refund the $2,000 overpayment to SSA within 30 days

A representative may not keep any amount above the authorized fee.

21. Which behavior may trigger representative sanctions?

  1. Respecting claimant instructions
  2. Accurately citing regulations
  3. Knowingly submitting false or misleading evidence
  4. Timely disclosing evidence
Show answer

C. Knowingly submitting false or misleading evidence

SSA regulates representative conduct and may sanction violations. Source: 20 CFR 404.1740; 416.1540.

22. Which of these is a prohibited action for a representative?

  1. Charging a fee that SSA has not authorized
  2. Withdrawing in writing before a hearing
  3. Asking SSA for an extension with good cause
  4. Getting written consent to share records
Show answer

A. Charging a fee that SSA has not authorized

Any fee for SSA representation needs SSA authorization, except payment from certain third parties in limited cases.

23. A representative learns that the claimant has been working full-time under the table while saying he does not work. The representative must

  1. not present false statements and should urge the claimant to correct the record; may need to withdraw
  2. guarantee the client the claim will still win, and say nothing to SSA about the part-time work
  3. report it publicly
  4. continue and stay silent
Show answer

A. not present false statements and should urge the claimant to correct the record; may need to withdraw

Knowingly participating in false statements violates 20 CFR 404.1740.

24. Who decides whether a representative may charge a fee for services before SSA?

  1. The representative alone
  2. The state bar or a state licensing board for representatives
  3. The claimant alone
  4. SSA, by approving a fee agreement or authorizing a fee petition
Show answer

D. SSA, by approving a fee agreement or authorizing a fee petition

No fee may be charged or collected for SSA representation without SSA authorization (with narrow exceptions).
