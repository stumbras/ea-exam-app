Practice questions with answers

1. Most of a motorcycle's stopping power comes from the

  1. Rear brake
  2. Front brake
  3. Clutch
  4. Engine
Show answer

B. Front brake

The front brake gives about 70% or more of your stopping power.

2. For the quickest safe stop, you should

  1. Use the rear brake only
  2. Pull in the clutch and coast
  3. Use both brakes at the same time
  4. Use the front brake only
Show answer

C. Use both brakes at the same time

Apply both smoothly and firmly without locking a wheel.

3. The single most important piece of protective gear is

  1. A bright jacket
  2. Leather gloves
  3. A helmet that meets DOT standards
  4. Riding boots
Show answer

C. A helmet that meets DOT standards

Head injuries are the main cause of rider deaths.

4. The best eye protection is

  1. A face shield
  2. Sunglasses
  3. A windshield alone
  4. Regular eyeglasses
Show answer

A. A face shield

A shield protects your whole face from wind, dust and insects.

5. To turn right at speed, you

  1. Press forward on the right handgrip
  2. Press forward on the left handgrip
  3. Turn the handlebars sharply right
  4. Lean your body only
Show answer

A. Press forward on the right handgrip

This is countersteering: press right, lean right, go right.

6. When approaching a curve, you should

  1. Brake hard in the middle of the turn
  2. Shift to neutral
  3. Speed up as you enter
  4. Slow down before the turn
Show answer

D. Slow down before the turn

Slow, look, press and roll on the throttle through the curve.

7. In a turn, you should look

  1. Through the turn to where you want to go
  2. At the outside edge
  3. At the road just ahead of the front wheel
  4. At your mirrors
Show answer

A. Through the turn to where you want to go

Turn your head, not just your eyes. The motorcycle goes where you look.

8. Most crashes between a car and a motorcycle happen

  1. On curves in the country
  2. On freeways
  3. In parking lots
  4. At intersections
Show answer

D. At intersections

The most common case is a car turning left in front of the rider.

9. To be seen by other drivers, you should

  1. Follow closely behind cars
  2. Ride in the right edge of the lane
  3. Wear bright clothing and keep your headlight on
  4. Ride only at night
Show answer

C. Wear bright clothing and keep your headlight on

Many drivers say they never saw the motorcycle.

10. Riding in a driver's blind spot is

  1. Dangerous, because the driver may change lanes into you
  2. The best place to be seen
  3. Required in heavy traffic
  4. Safe if you match their speed
Show answer

A. Dangerous, because the driver may change lanes into you

Move ahead or drop back so you appear in the mirrors.

11. A car and a motorcycle in the same lane

  1. Can share the lane side by side
  2. Is required on freeways
  3. Should share if traffic is slow
  4. Each need a full lane width to be safe
Show answer

D. Each need a full lane width to be safe

Do not let drivers squeeze beside you. Ride where you protect your lane.

12. The road is most slippery

  1. Once the rain stops
  2. Only when it snows
  3. After an hour of rain
  4. During the first few minutes of rain
Show answer

D. During the first few minutes of rain

Oil and dirt mix with the first water and make the surface slick.

13. On a slippery surface, you should

  1. Speed up to get across
  2. Brake hard with the front brake
  3. Lean more in turns
  4. Slow down and avoid sudden moves
Show answer

D. Slow down and avoid sudden moves

Keep the motorcycle as upright as you can.

14. You should cross railroad tracks that run across your path at an angle of

  1. Any angle at high speed
  2. Parallel to the tracks
  3. As close to 90 degrees as is safe, and at least 45
  4. Less than 20 degrees
Show answer

C. As close to 90 degrees as is safe, and at least 45

A shallow angle can catch your tire.

15. If you must ride over an object in the road, you should

  1. Lean far to one side
  2. Pull in the clutch and coast
  3. Rise slightly off the seat and keep a firm grip
  4. Brake hard as you hit it
Show answer

C. Rise slightly off the seat and keep a firm grip

Approach as straight as you can and roll on the throttle slightly just before contact.

16. If your front wheel locks while braking, you should

  1. Release the front brake at once, then reapply it smoothly
  2. Apply more rear brake
  3. Keep it locked until you stop
  4. Turn the handlebars
Show answer

A. Release the front brake at once, then reapply it smoothly

A locked front wheel will make you fall very quickly.

17. Your motorcycle starts to wobble. You should

  1. Accelerate out of it
  2. Fight it by steering back and forth
  3. Grip the bars firmly and close the throttle slowly
  4. Brake hard
Show answer

C. Grip the bars firmly and close the throttle slowly

Do not brake or speed up. Slow gradually, then pull off and check the load and tires.

18. If a tire goes flat while riding, you should

  1. Hold the grips firmly and ease off the throttle
  2. Brake hard on the flat tire
  3. Shift to a higher gear
  4. Swerve to the shoulder at once
Show answer

A. Hold the grips firmly and ease off the throttle

If you must brake, gently use the brake on the tire that is not flat.

19. If your throttle sticks, first

  1. Jump off the motorcycle
  2. Shift up a gear
  3. Twist it back and forth to free it
  4. Brake as hard as you can
Show answer

C. Twist it back and forth to free it

If that fails, use the engine cut-off switch and pull in the clutch together.

20. When you must swerve to avoid a hazard, you should

  1. Stand on the pegs
  2. Not brake while you are swerving
  3. Brake hard during the swerve
  4. Close your eyes and lean
Show answer

B. Not brake while you are swerving

Brake before or after the swerve, never during it.

21. A passenger should

  1. Lean with you and keep feet on the footrests
  2. Put feet down at stops
  3. Lean the opposite way in turns
  4. Sit as far back as possible
Show answer

A. Lean with you and keep feet on the footrests

Tell your passenger to hold on, sit still and keep feet up even when stopped.

22. Carrying a passenger makes the motorcycle

  1. Easier to balance at low speed
  2. Slower to stop, speed up and turn
  3. Handle the same
  4. Stop faster
Show answer

B. Slower to stop, speed up and turn

Ride slower and leave more space.

23. The best formation for a group on a straight road is

  1. Side by side in pairs
  2. Bunched close together
  3. Staggered
  4. Single file at all times
Show answer

C. Staggered

Staggering keeps the group close while giving each rider space.

24. At night you should

  1. Wear a tinted shield
  2. Ride closer to the car ahead to see better
  3. Slow down and leave more space ahead
  4. Use the low beam only
Show answer

C. Slow down and leave more space ahead

Use your high beam when no one is ahead or coming toward you.

25. A dog runs at your motorcycle. You should

  1. Swerve into the next lane
  2. Stop and wait
  3. Kick at the dog
  4. Slow down, then speed up as you get near it
Show answer

D. Slow down, then speed up as you get near it

Slowing and then accelerating throws off the dog's timing.

26. Alcohol first affects your

  1. Strength
  2. Hearing
  3. Vision only
  4. Judgment
Show answer

D. Judgment

Riding takes more skill than driving, and alcohol hits those skills early.

27. Before every ride you should check

  1. The paint
  2. Only the fuel level
  3. The seat height
  4. Tires, controls, lights, oil and chain
Show answer

D. Tires, controls, lights, oil and chain

A quick walk-around catches problems before they cause a crash.

28. For protection, besides a helmet you should wear

  1. A scarf and sneakers
  2. Shorts and sandals in summer
  3. A jacket, long pants, gloves and boots that cover the ankle
  4. Whatever is comfortable
Show answer

C. A jacket, long pants, gloves and boots that cover the ankle

Good gear protects your skin in a slide and helps drivers see you.

29. When parked at a curb, your motorcycle should be

  1. On the sidewalk
  2. At an angle with the rear wheel touching the curb
  3. Parallel, like a car
  4. With the front wheel against the curb
Show answer

B. At an angle with the rear wheel touching the curb

Parking at an angle lets you pull out safely.

30. When you slow to a stop, you should shift

  1. Down through the gears so you are in first
  2. Only after you have stopped
  3. Up one gear
  4. Into neutral early
Show answer

A. Down through the gears so you are in first

Staying in first lets you move off quickly if a vehicle comes up behind you.

31. In traffic, the minimum following distance behind the vehicle ahead should be

  1. One car length
  2. One second
  3. Two seconds
  4. Half a second
Show answer

C. Two seconds

A two-second gap is the minimum. Leave more at night, in rain, or when you cannot see through the vehicle ahead.

32. A lane gives a motorcycle how many paths of travel?

  1. Two, left and right
  2. One, in the center
  3. Four
  4. Three: left, center and right
Show answer

D. Three: left, center and right

Think of the lane as three positions and pick the one where you are seen best and have room to move.

33. The best lane position is the one that

  1. Keeps you beside other vehicles
  2. Lets you be seen and gives you space to avoid hazards
  3. Is always the center
  4. Is always next to the curb
Show answer

B. Lets you be seen and gives you space to avoid hazards

No single position is always best. Change position as traffic and the road change.

34. The oily strip in the center of a lane is usually

  1. Wider than the tire tracks
  2. Too slippery to ever ride on
  3. The only safe place to ride
  4. Safe to ride on unless the road is wet
Show answer

D. Safe to ride on unless the road is wet

The center strip is usually no more than two feet wide. Avoid it in the first rain and at intersections where oil builds up.

35. At an intersection, the greatest danger to a rider is usually

  1. A parked car
  2. An oncoming car turning left across your path
  3. A car stopped behind you
  4. A car in the far lane going your way
Show answer

B. An oncoming car turning left across your path

Drivers turning left often fail to see a motorcycle. Slow down and be ready to stop or swerve.

36. When you approach an intersection where a car could pull out, you should

  1. Speed up to get through quickly
  2. Slow down and choose a lane position that makes you visible
  3. Move to the edge of the road and stop
  4. Sound your horn and hold your speed
Show answer

B. Slow down and choose a lane position that makes you visible

Cover the brakes and give yourself space. Never count on eye contact to mean the driver sees you.

37. Making eye contact with a driver means

  1. The driver will yield
  2. Nothing certain: the driver may still pull out
  3. You have the right of way
  4. It is safe to speed up
Show answer

B. Nothing certain: the driver may still pull out

Drivers often look right at a motorcycle and still fail to see it.

38. When passing parked cars, you should ride

  1. On the shoulder
  2. In the left part of your lane
  3. As close to the cars as you can
  4. In the center of the lane only
Show answer

B. In the left part of your lane

Staying left gives room for opening doors and for people stepping out between cars.

39. Before changing lanes you should

  1. Check your mirrors and turn your head to check the blind spot
  2. Signal and move at once
  3. Check your mirrors only
  4. Sound your horn
Show answer

A. Check your mirrors and turn your head to check the blind spot

Motorcycle mirrors do not show everything. A head check is the only way to see a vehicle beside you.

40. Objects in a convex mirror appear

  1. Upside down
  2. Farther away than they are
  3. Exactly as far as they are
  4. Closer than they are
Show answer

B. Farther away than they are

Convex mirrors show more of the road but make vehicles look farther away. Get used to them before you rely on them.

41. Flashing your brake light before you slow down is most useful when

  1. Someone is following closely or you are slowing where others do not expect it
  2. You are parked
  3. You are speeding up
  4. You are riding alone on an empty road
Show answer

A. Someone is following closely or you are slowing where others do not expect it

A few light taps on the brake warn the driver behind you.

42. Leaving a turn signal on after you finish a turn is

  1. Fine in light traffic
  2. Required at night
  3. Dangerous: a driver may think you are turning and pull out in front of you
  4. Safer than cancelling it
Show answer

C. Dangerous: a driver may think you are turning and pull out in front of you

Many motorcycle signals do not cancel themselves. Check them after every turn or lane change.

43. To stop quickly in a curve, the best method is to

  1. Straighten the motorcycle first if you can, then brake hard
  2. Shift to neutral
  3. Brake as hard as possible while leaned over
  4. Use only the rear brake
Show answer

A. Straighten the motorcycle first if you can, then brake hard

If you cannot straighten up, brake smoothly and increase pressure as the motorcycle straightens.

44. If you lock the rear wheel on a good surface, you should

  1. Shift up a gear
  2. Keep it locked until you have stopped
  3. Lean hard to one side
  4. Release it and stamp on it again
Show answer

B. Keep it locked until you have stopped

Releasing a locked rear wheel while the motorcycle is out of line can throw you off. Keep it locked and steer straight.

45. Rolling on the throttle slightly through a turn

  1. Should only be done on hills
  2. Helps keep the motorcycle stable
  3. Is dangerous on dry roads
  4. Makes the motorcycle fall inward
Show answer

B. Helps keep the motorcycle stable

Steady or gently increasing throttle settles the suspension through the curve.

46. In a slow, tight turn you should

  1. Stand on the footrests
  2. Brake hard with the front brake
  3. Lean your body far into the turn
  4. Keep your body upright and let the motorcycle lean
Show answer

D. Keep your body upright and let the motorcycle lean

In slow turns, counterbalance by keeping your body straight while the motorcycle leans.

47. When starting on a hill, you should

  1. Rev the engine and release the clutch fast
  2. Roll backward first to gain room
  3. Hold the motorcycle with the front brake, then switch to the rear brake as you ease out the clutch
  4. Use no brakes at all
Show answer

C. Hold the motorcycle with the front brake, then switch to the rear brake as you ease out the clutch

Using the brake keeps you from rolling back while you find the friction zone.

48. Crossing metal grooves or bridge gratings, you should

  1. Put both feet down
  2. Zigzag across them
  3. Relax, keep a steady speed and ride straight across
  4. Brake hard
Show answer

C. Relax, keep a steady speed and ride straight across

The weaving feeling is normal. Fighting it or riding at an angle makes it worse.

49. Tracks or pavement seams that run in the same direction you are going should be crossed at an angle of at least

  1. 5 degrees
  2. 45 degrees
  3. 10 degrees
  4. Zero: ride along them
Show answer

B. 45 degrees

Move far enough away to cross at 45 degrees or more so your tire does not catch the edge.

50. If you ride onto a patch of ice or very slick surface you cannot avoid, you should

  1. Keep the motorcycle upright, squeeze the clutch and coast
  2. Lean sharply
  3. Brake hard
  4. Accelerate across it
Show answer

A. Keep the motorcycle upright, squeeze the clutch and coast

Stay off the brakes and avoid any sudden move until you are across.

51. If the drive chain breaks or locks, you should

  1. Shift down quickly
  2. Speed up
  3. Keep riding to the next exit
  4. Close the throttle and brake to a stop
Show answer

D. Close the throttle and brake to a stop

A broken chain means no drive to the rear wheel. A locked chain can lock the wheel, so stop.

52. If the engine seizes, you should

  1. Shift up
  2. Squeeze the clutch to disconnect the engine and pull off the road
  3. Brake with the rear only
  4. Open the throttle
Show answer

B. Squeeze the clutch to disconnect the engine and pull off the road

Squeezing the clutch frees the rear wheel so you can coast to a safe stop.

53. If something hits your face shield or you get an insect in your eye, you should

  1. Brake hard immediately
  2. Wipe it off at once with both hands
  3. Close both eyes
  4. Keep your eyes on the road and your hands on the bars, then pull over when it is safe
Show answer

D. Keep your eyes on the road and your hands on the bars, then pull over when it is safe

Stay in control first. Fix the problem after you have stopped safely.

54. A windshield on the motorcycle is

  1. Enough eye protection by itself
  2. A replacement for a helmet
  3. Only needed at night
  4. Not a substitute for a face shield or goggles
Show answer

D. Not a substitute for a face shield or goggles

Most windshields do not keep wind and debris out of your eyes.

55. A plastic face shield should be replaced when it is

  1. Clean
  2. Scratched enough to blur your vision
  3. One month old
  4. Tinted
Show answer

B. Scratched enough to blur your vision

Scratches scatter light and can blind you at night or in sun.

56. Which helmet gives the most protection?

  1. A half helmet
  2. A bicycle helmet
  3. A full-face helmet that meets DOT standards
  4. A cap worn under any helmet
Show answer

C. A full-face helmet that meets DOT standards

A full-face helmet protects the chin and face as well as the skull. It must fit snugly and be fastened.

57. Wearing a helmet

  1. Does not block the hearing or side vision you need to ride safely
  2. Makes crashes more likely
  3. Is only useful on highways
  4. Blocks most of your hearing
Show answer

A. Does not block the hearing or side vision you need to ride safely

Studies of riders show helmets do not keep you from seeing to the sides or hearing traffic.

58. When you carry a passenger, you should tell them to

  1. Mount before you do
  2. Keep their feet down at stops
  3. Get on only after the engine is started and sit as far forward as they can without crowding you
  4. Lean the opposite way in turns
Show answer

C. Get on only after the engine is started and sit as far forward as they can without crowding you

Give the passenger instructions before you start, even if they ride themselves.

59. With a passenger or a heavy load, you should

  1. Remove a mirror for space
  2. Add tire pressure and adjust the suspension as the owner's manual says
  3. Lower the tire pressure
  4. Change nothing
Show answer

B. Add tire pressure and adjust the suspension as the owner's manual says

Extra weight changes handling. Check the manual for the right pressure and settings.

60. A load should be carried

  1. On the handlebars
  2. Behind the rear axle only
  3. High on a sissy bar
  4. Low and forward, over or ahead of the rear axle
Show answer

D. Low and forward, over or ahead of the rear axle

Weight carried high or far back upsets the balance and can cause a wobble.

61. In a group, the least experienced riders should ride

  1. At the very back
  2. Just behind the leader
  3. Beside the leader
  4. Wherever they like
Show answer

B. Just behind the leader

Behind the leader, more experienced riders can watch them and the group will not outrun them.

62. Groups of riders should keep

  1. As large as possible
  2. Side by side in each lane
  3. Small, so they are easier to pass and less likely to be split up
  4. In single file at all times
Show answer

C. Small, so they are easier to pass and less likely to be split up

If a group is larger than four or five, split it into smaller groups.

63. Riding side by side with another motorcycle in one lane is

  1. The best way to be seen
  2. Unsafe, because neither rider has room to move
  3. Required in groups
  4. Safest at night
Show answer

B. Unsafe, because neither rider has room to move

Each rider needs the full lane to swerve or avoid a hazard.

64. In a staggered group formation, the second rider follows the leader by about

  1. Half a second, directly behind
  2. One second, in the other side of the lane
  3. Three seconds, directly behind
  4. Ten seconds
Show answer

B. One second, in the other side of the lane

The stagger keeps riders close but gives each a two-second gap behind the rider directly ahead.

65. A group should pass another vehicle

  1. One rider at a time
  2. All together
  3. On the shoulder
  4. Two at a time side by side
Show answer

A. One rider at a time

Each rider completes the pass and returns to position before the next one goes.

66. When riding at night, you should use your high beam

  1. Only in rain
  2. Only in town
  3. Never
  4. Whenever you are not following or meeting another vehicle
Show answer

D. Whenever you are not following or meeting another vehicle

High beams let you see farther. Dim them for oncoming traffic and when following.

67. At night, the taillights of the car ahead can help you by

  1. Showing dips and bumps in the road as they bounce
  2. Lighting the roadside
  3. Warming your tires
  4. Showing your speed
Show answer

A. Showing dips and bumps in the road as they bounce

Watching the lights ahead gives early warning of the road surface.

68. The body gets rid of alcohol at about

  1. A rate that coffee can speed up
  2. One drink per hour
  3. Three drinks per hour
  4. One drink every ten minutes
Show answer

B. One drink per hour

Only time lowers your blood alcohol. Coffee, food and cold showers do not.

69. Compared with driving a car, riding a motorcycle after drinking is

  1. More dangerous, because riding needs more balance and coordination
  2. Safer, because the motorcycle is smaller
  3. About the same
  4. Safer at low speed
Show answer

A. More dangerous, because riding needs more balance and coordination

Even a small amount of alcohol reduces the skills riding depends on.

70. To reduce fatigue on a long ride, you should

  1. Take a rest break at least every two hours and limit how far you go in a day
  2. Skip meals
  3. Ride faster to finish sooner
  4. Avoid wind protection
Show answer

A. Take a rest break at least every two hours and limit how far you go in a day

Wind, cold and vibration tire you faster on a motorcycle than in a car.

71. Before you ride an unfamiliar motorcycle, you should

  1. Remove the mirrors
  2. Ignore the owner's manual
  3. Find and try all the controls and ride more cautiously at first
  4. Ride it at highway speed right away
Show answer

C. Find and try all the controls and ride more cautiously at first

Many crashes happen on a motorcycle the rider has had for less than six months.

72. The front tire should be checked before each ride for

  1. Brand
  2. Air pressure, wear and damage
  3. Age only
  4. Color
Show answer

B. Air pressure, wear and damage

A tire with low pressure or damage can fail or handle badly.
