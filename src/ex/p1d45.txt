130|Income is taxed in layers. Each rate applies only to the income inside its layer, not to all your income. Example for a single person with taxable income of $60,000: 10% on the first $11,925, 12% on the next $36,550, and 22% only on the last $11,525.
131|The joint brackets for the lower rates are exactly double the single brackets: the 10% bracket ends at $23,850 and the 12% bracket at $96,950. Only income above each line is taxed at the next rate.
132|The kiddie tax covers children under 18, and also 18-year-olds and full-time students aged 19 to 23 who do not earn more than half of their own support. At least one parent must be alive, and the child must not file a joint return.
133|Form 8615 is attached to the child's own return. It figures the tax on the child's unearned income above $2,700 using the parents' tax rate.
134|In place of a separate return for the child, parents may add the child's income to their own return with Form 8814. It is allowed only if the child had just interest and dividends, under $13,500, with no tax withheld or estimated payments. It is simpler but raises the parents' AGI.
135|The AMT is a second way of figuring tax that allows fewer deductions. The exemption is an amount of AMT income that is free of AMT, which keeps most people out of it: $88,100 single and $137,000 joint for 2025.
136|The AMT exemption is taken away from very high earners. For each dollar of AMT income above $626,350 single or $1,252,700 joint, the exemption drops by 25 cents.
137|The AMT has two rates. 26% applies to the first $239,100 of AMT income after the exemption, and 28% to the rest. The result is compared with regular tax, and you pay the higher one.
138|To figure the AMT you add back items the regular tax allows. State and local taxes and the standard deduction are not allowed. The bargain on exercising incentive stock options is added as income, and so is interest on private activity municipal bonds.
139|A self-employed person pays both halves of Social Security and Medicare tax. The half that an employer would have paid is deducted as an adjustment to income. It lowers income tax, not the self-employment tax itself.
140|High earners pay an extra 0.9% Medicare tax on wages and self-employment income above $200,000 single or $250,000 joint. An employer starts withholding once wages pass $200,000 no matter the filing status, so the right amount is settled on Form 8959.
141|Tax must be paid as income is earned. If you expect to owe at least $1,000 after subtracting withholding and credits, you must make quarterly estimated payments. This mostly affects the self-employed, landlords and investors.
142|Estimated payments are due four times, and the periods are not equal: April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15 of the next year. When a date falls on a weekend it moves to the next business day, which is why June 16 applied in 2025.
143|Farm and fishing income often arrives once a year. If at least two-thirds of gross income comes from farming or fishing, only one estimated payment is needed, by January 15. It can be skipped completely by filing the return and paying all tax by March 1.
144|Form 2210 figures the penalty for paying too little estimated tax. Most people can let the IRS compute it. You file it yourself to ask for a waiver or to use the annualized income method.
145|Filing late costs 5% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month, up to 25%. It is based on tax not paid, so a late return with a refund has no penalty. If the delay is fraudulent the rate is 15% a month, up to 75%.
146|Paying late costs 0.5% of the unpaid tax per month, up to 25%. It is ten times smaller than the late filing penalty, so always file on time even if you cannot pay. The rate drops to 0.25% during an installment agreement and rises to 1% after a levy notice.
147|When a return is both late and unpaid, the two penalties are not simply added. The late filing penalty is reduced by the late payment penalty for that month, so the total is 5% a month: 4.5% for filing and 0.5% for paying.
148|When a return understates tax because of carelessness or a large error, the penalty is 20% of the underpaid tax. For an individual, large means the understatement is more than the greater of 10% of the correct tax or $5,000.
149|A person who pays a nanny, housekeeper or caregiver can be an employer. If cash wages to one worker reach $2,800 in 2025, Social Security and Medicare taxes are due. If total wages reach $1,000 in any quarter, federal unemployment tax is due. Both are reported on Schedule H with Form 1040.
150|Interest on unpaid tax is set each quarter: the federal short-term rate plus 3 percentage points, compounded daily. It runs from the original due date, even during an extension, and normally cannot be waived.
151|Keep records as long as the IRS can still question the return. Usually that is 3 years from filing. It is 6 years if more than 25% of income was left off, and there is no limit if no return was filed or it was fraudulent. Keep property records until 3 years after you sell the property.
152|Employers must keep payroll tax records, such as wages paid and deposits made, for at least 4 years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later.
153|To get money back you must claim it within 3 years from the date the return was filed, or 2 years from the date the tax was paid, whichever is later. A return filed early counts as filed on the due date.
154|To correct a return already filed, an individual files Form 1040-X. It shows the original amounts, the changes and the corrected amounts, with an explanation. It can be filed electronically.
155|Property moved between spouses, or to a former spouse as part of a divorce, is treated like a gift for income tax: no gain or loss, and the receiver takes over the giver's basis. So the built-in gain moves to the one who receives the property.
156|The IRS matches the name and Social Security number on a return against Social Security records. After a name change, tell the Social Security Administration first; if not, an e-filed return is rejected or the refund is delayed.
157|Withholding is set by a form given to the payer. Employees use Form W-4. People receiving regular pension payments use Form W-4P, and Form W-4R is for one-time retirement payments.
158|Marital status is decided on December 31. A person whose divorce is final by that date is unmarried for the entire year, and files as Single, or as Head of Household if they qualify.
258|The law still says people should have health coverage, but the federal penalty for not having it has been $0 since 2019. Nothing is owed on the federal return. Some states have their own penalty.
259|Sometimes an employer cannot collect the employee's share of Social Security and Medicare tax on tips because wages were too small. The unpaid amount appears in box 12 with codes A and B, and the employee pays it on the return through Schedule 2.
260|Form 4868 gives 6 more months to file, to October 15. It does not give more time to pay. Tax not paid by April 15 is charged interest and the late payment penalty, so you estimate what you owe and pay with the extension.
261|Normally no tax is withheld from interest or dividends. If you do not give the payer a correct tax ID number, or the IRS says you underreported, the payer must hold back 24% and send it to the IRS. You claim it as a payment on your return.
262|Bonuses, commissions and similar extra pay can have income tax withheld at a flat 22%. Once extra pay passes $1 million in a year, the part above that must be withheld at 37%.
263|Schedule 2 collects taxes that are added on top of the regular income tax: the AMT, repayment of excess advance premium credit, self-employment tax, the 10% tax on early retirement distributions, household employment tax, Additional Medicare Tax and the net investment income tax.
264|Two people with similar high incomes can owe more tax married than they would as two single people: a marriage penalty. When one spouse earns most of the income, the joint brackets usually lower the total: a marriage bonus.
265|Filing separately usually costs more, but it can help in a few cases: one spouse does not want to be liable for the other's tax, one spouse has large medical bills measured against a smaller AGI, or student loan payments are based on separate income.
266|If too little or too much tax is being taken from pay, the fix is a new Form W-4 given to the employer. The IRS online estimator helps choose the entries. People with income that has no withholding make estimated payments with Form 1040-ES.
267|Buying a home changes the return. Mortgage interest and property tax may now exceed the standard deduction, and points on the purchase may be deductible. The closing statement and records of improvements should be kept because they set the basis for a later sale.
268|A death creates several filings: a final Form 1040, perhaps Form 1041 for the estate's income and Form 706 for a large estate. The survivor may file jointly that year. Heirs take inherited property at its date-of-death value, but income the decedent had earned is taxed to whoever receives it.
269|Several tax benefits help pay for education: 529 plans, Coverdell accounts, the savings bond interest exclusion, the two education credits and the student loan interest deduction. The same dollar of expense can be used for only one benefit, so they must be planned together.
307|The normal penalty calculation assumes income came in evenly through the year. If most income came late in the year, for example a December bonus or a business with a busy season, Schedule AI figures required payments from actual income in each period, which lowers or removes the penalty.
308|If a return is more than 60 days late, there is a minimum late filing penalty: for 2025 returns (due in 2026) it is $525, or 100% of the unpaid tax if that is smaller. So even a small balance can bring a noticeable penalty.
334|A minister is an employee for income tax and gets a Form W-2, but for Social Security the minister is treated as self-employed. No Social Security or Medicare tax is withheld; the minister pays self-employment tax on salary plus housing allowance. A minister opposed on religious grounds can ask for exemption with Form 4361.
682|Form 1040-X corrects a return that was already filed, for example to add income, change filing status or claim a missed deduction. To get a refund it must be filed within 3 years of the original filing or 2 years of payment.
683|Form 1040-ES contains a worksheet to estimate the year's tax and vouchers for the four quarterly payments. It is used by people whose income has no withholding.
686|Schedule 2 has two parts. Part I has the AMT and repayment of excess advance premium tax credit. Part II has other taxes such as self-employment tax, the additional tax on retirement accounts, household employment tax, Additional Medicare Tax and the 3.8% net investment income tax.
695|Schedule H is attached to Form 1040 by someone who employs household help. It reports Social Security, Medicare and federal unemployment taxes on the worker's wages, so the employer pays them with the personal return.
696|Farm and fishing income can swing from year to year. Schedule J lets the farmer spread part of this year's farm income over the 3 earlier years' tax rates, which lowers tax in a good year. The earlier returns are not amended.
698|Schedule SE figures self-employment tax: 15.3% of 92.35% of net profit (12.4% Social Security up to the wage base and 2.9% Medicare). It is required when net self-employment earnings are $400 or more.
703|An employee fills in Form W-4 so the employer knows how much income tax to take from each paycheck. It reflects filing status, other jobs, dependents and other adjustments. A new one can be given at any time.
752|Form 6251 figures the alternative minimum tax. It starts with taxable income, adds back the AMT adjustments, subtracts the exemption and applies the 26% and 28% rates. You pay AMT only if the result is more than regular tax.
753|Form 8959 figures the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages and self-employment income above the threshold, and compares it with what employers withheld.
754|Form 8960 figures the 3.8% net investment income tax. It totals investment income, subtracts related expenses, and applies the tax to the smaller of that amount or MAGI above the threshold.
755|Form 4868 gives an automatic 6-month extension to file Form 1040, until October 15. No reason is needed. It must be filed by the original due date, and it does not extend the time to pay.
756|Form 9465 asks the IRS for a monthly payment plan when the tax cannot be paid in full. Interest and a reduced late payment penalty keep running until the balance is paid.
757|Form 8857 asks the IRS to relieve one spouse from tax on a joint return that was caused by the other spouse's errors. It is generally due within 2 years after the IRS first tries to collect from the requesting spouse.
758|Form 8379 is filed by a spouse whose share of a joint refund was, or will be, taken to pay the other spouse's past debts, such as child support or a student loan. It splits the refund so the injured spouse gets their part.
834|The alternative minimum tax makes sure people with many deductions still pay some tax. You figure tax a second way, with fewer deductions and an exemption, at 26% and 28%. If this is more than the regular tax, you pay the difference as AMT.
835|Alternative minimum taxable income is taxable income after adding back the items the AMT does not allow, such as state and local taxes and the bargain on incentive stock options. The exemption is subtracted from it before applying the AMT rates.
836|FICA is the payroll tax law. The employee pays 6.2% Social Security (up to the wage base, $176,100 for 2025) and 1.45% Medicare, and the employer pays the same. Self-employed people pay both halves as self-employment tax.
863|The marginal rate is the rate on your next dollar of income, meaning the top bracket you reach. It tells you how much a deduction saves. Example: in the 22% bracket, a $1,000 deduction saves $220.
864|The effective rate is your average rate: total tax divided by income. Because lower layers of income are taxed at lower rates, it is always below the marginal rate. Example: $9,000 of tax on $75,000 of income is 12%.
865|Withholding is tax taken out of a payment before you receive it, mainly from wages. It is treated as paid evenly through the year, whenever it was actually withheld, which helps avoid the estimated tax penalty.
866|A safe harbor is a clear test that protects you from a penalty if you meet it, no matter how things turn out. The best known: no estimated tax penalty if you paid at least 100% of last year's tax (110% for higher incomes) or 90% of this year's.
867|The statute of limitations is a deadline in the law. The IRS generally has 3 years from filing to assess more tax, and a taxpayer has 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment to claim a refund. After that, the year is closed.
868|On a joint return each spouse is fully responsible for all the tax, interest and penalties, even on income the other spouse earned, and even after a divorce. Innocent spouse relief is the exception to this rule.
