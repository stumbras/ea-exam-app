659|An individual return is due April 15. Form 4868, filed by that date, gives an automatic 6 months, to October 15. The extension is only for filing: the tax must still be paid by April 15 or interest and the late payment penalty apply.
660|A citizen or resident who lives and works outside the United States on April 15 gets 2 extra months, to June 15, without filing anything; a statement is attached to the return. Interest still runs from April 15. A further extension to October 15 can be requested.
661|A return or payment is on time if it is postmarked by the due date, even if the IRS receives it later. This applies to the U.S. Postal Service and to approved private delivery services. If the due date is a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day.
662|Pass-through entities file first so owners get their information: partnerships and S corporations on March 15. C corporations and estates and trusts file on April 15. Exempt organizations file on May 15. Business extensions use Form 7004; exempt organizations use Form 8868.
663|Forms W-2 and 1099-NEC are due January 31 both to the worker and to the government. Most other Forms 1099 go to recipients by January 31, and to the IRS by February 28 on paper or March 31 if filed electronically.
664|A filer with 10 or more information returns in a year must file them electronically. All types are added together to reach 10: W-2, 1099, 1098 and others.
665|Form 8809 asks for 30 more days to file information returns with the IRS. For Forms W-2 and 1099-NEC the extension is not automatic and is granted only for specific reasons.
666|Anyone paid to prepare, or help prepare, all or most of a federal tax return must have a PTIN and renew it each year. It goes on every return prepared, in place of the preparer's Social Security number.
667|A firm that wants to e-file returns for clients applies to the IRS and passes a suitability check. It then receives an EFIN, which identifies the firm on every return it transmits. It is for the firm; a PTIN is for the individual preparer.
668|The electronic return originator is the business that starts the electronic filing of a return, after the taxpayer authorizes it. The originator is responsible for the signature authorization, timely transmission and safeguarding of taxpayer data.
669|Publication 1345 is the rulebook for e-file providers of individual returns. It covers signatures, how to handle rejected returns, record keeping, refund products and advertising standards.
670|With Form 8879 the taxpayer authorizes the e-file provider to enter or generate the taxpayer's PIN as the electronic signature. It must be signed before the return is sent. The provider keeps it for 3 years and does not send it to the IRS unless asked.
671|Some returns need paper attachments that cannot be e-filed. Form 8453 is the cover sheet used to mail those documents to the IRS after the electronic return is accepted.
672|If the IRS rejects an e-filed return, the provider must tell the taxpayer within 24 hours. The return is still on time if corrected and sent again within 5 days. If it cannot be fixed, a paper return is on time if mailed by the due date or within 10 days of the rejection notice, whichever is later.
673|A preparer or firm that expects to file 11 or more individual or trust income tax returns in a year must e-file them. If a client insists on paper, the preparer gets a signed statement from the client and attaches Form 8948 to the paper return.
674|A refund can be split among up to 3 accounts with Form 8888, and no more than 3 refunds a year can go into one account. A preparer may not have a client's refund deposited into the preparer's own account.
675|An Identity Protection PIN is a 6-digit number that changes each year. If a taxpayer has one, the return will not be accepted without it. Any taxpayer who can verify their identity may ask for one, not only victims of identity theft.
676|Every paid preparer has four basic duties for each return: sign it, enter the PTIN, give the taxpayer a complete copy, and keep a copy or a list of taxpayers for 3 years. Each failure has its own penalty.
677|For preparer record-keeping rules, the return period is not the calendar year. It is the 12 months starting July 1 and ending June 30. Records are kept 3 years from the end of the return period in which the return was presented for signing.
678|A Certifying Acceptance Agent has an agreement with the IRS to review an ITIN applicant's passport and other identity documents and certify them. The applicant then does not need to mail the originals to the IRS with Form W-7.
679|Form 1040-X can be filed electronically for the current tax year and the 2 years before it. Older years must be amended on paper.
977|Form 8633 was the paper application to become an authorized e-file provider. The application is now completed online through IRS e-Services.
978|When a preparer who is required to e-file submits a paper return, Form 8948 is attached to explain why, for example because the client chose to file on paper.
979|Form 8888 lets a taxpayer divide a refund among two or three accounts, such as checking, savings and an IRA.
980|Form 9325 can be given to a taxpayer as confirmation that the IRS received and accepted the e-filed return or extension.
981|Form 8878 is the signature authorization used when an e-file provider files an extension, Form 4868, with an electronic funds withdrawal for the taxpayer.
1023|An ETIN identifies a business that transmits electronic returns directly to the IRS. It is different from the EFIN, which identifies the provider that originates the returns.
1024|The IP PIN is a 6-digit number the IRS issues each year. A return filed with the taxpayer's Social Security number but without the correct IP PIN is rejected, which blocks an identity thief from filing.
1025|A Certifying Acceptance Agent is authorized by the IRS to verify the identity and foreign status documents of people applying for an ITIN and to send the application for them.
1026|Modernized e-File is the IRS's current system for receiving electronic returns. It accepts returns all year and returns an acknowledgment showing whether each one was accepted or rejected.
