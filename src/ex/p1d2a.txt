29|Social Security is taxed only when your other income is high enough. The base amount is the first line: below it nothing is taxable. $25,000 for a single person, $32,000 for a joint return. A married person filing separately who lived with the spouse has a base of $0, so benefits are almost always taxable.
30|However high the income, at most 85% of Social Security benefits is included in income. At least 15% is always tax-free. Example: $20,000 of benefits: no more than $17,000 can be taxable.
31|There are two steps. Above the first base amount, up to 50% of benefits is taxable. Above the second line ($34,000 single, $44,000 joint) up to 85% is taxable. These amounts are not adjusted for inflation.
32|The date the divorce was signed decides the rule. Before 2019: the payer deducts alimony and the receiver reports it as income. From 2019 on: no deduction and no income. An old agreement keeps the old rule unless it is changed and says the new rule applies.
33|Child support is for the child, not income to the parent who receives it. It is never deducted and never reported. If a payer falls behind, payments count as child support first, then alimony.
34|Money paid by a life insurance policy because the insured person died is not income to the beneficiary. If the insurer holds the money and pays it later with interest, only the interest is taxable.
35|Receiving a gift or inheritance is not income. But what the property earns afterward is taxable to the new owner. Example: inherit $100,000: not taxed. Put it in the bank and earn $4,000 of interest: taxed.
36|A scholarship is tax-free only for costs of the education itself: tuition, fees, and required books and supplies, and only for a student working toward a degree. The part that pays for room, food or travel is taxable income.
37|Unemployment benefits replace wages, so they are fully taxable. The state reports them on Form 1099-G. No Social Security tax is due on them, and they are not earned income.
38|A state refund is taxable only if deducting that state tax lowered your federal tax last year. If you took the standard deduction last year, the refund is not taxable. If your deduction was cut off by the SALT cap, part or all of the refund may also be non-taxable.
39|All gambling winnings are income, reported in full. Losses are deducted separately, only if you itemize, and only up to the amount of winnings. You cannot show just the net. Example: won $5,000, lost $8,000: report $5,000 income and deduct at most $5,000.
40|Qualified dividends get the lower capital gain rates, but only if you really held the stock. You must hold it more than 60 days during the 121 days around the ex-dividend date. Buying just before a dividend and selling just after gives an ordinary dividend.
41|Long-term gains and qualified dividends are taxed at 0% while taxable income stays under a threshold: $48,350 single, $96,700 joint. Example: a single person with taxable income of $40,000 including $5,000 of long-term gain pays no tax on that gain.
42|The top long-term rate, 20%, applies only to the part of taxable income above these high thresholds, such as $533,400 single and $600,050 joint. Between the 0% and 20% lines the rate is 15%.
43|Two kinds of long-term gain have higher maximum rates. Gain on collectibles such as coins, art and stamps: up to 28%. Gain on real estate that comes from depreciation previously deducted (unrecaptured Section 1250 gain): up to 25%.
44|Capital losses first offset capital gains without limit. If losses are larger, only $3,000 a year ($1,500 married filing separately) can reduce other income like wages. The rest carries forward with no time limit. Example: net loss $10,000: deduct $3,000, carry $7,000.
45|Hold an asset more than one year and the gain is long-term, taxed at lower rates. Exactly one year is still short-term. Count from the day after you bought it. Bought March 1, 2024: long-term starting March 2, 2025.
46|Gain on selling your main home can be left out of income: up to $250,000, or $500,000 on a joint return. For the $500,000, one spouse must have owned the home for 2 years and both must have lived in it for 2 years.
47|To exclude the gain you must have owned the home and lived in it as your main home for at least 2 of the 5 years before the sale. The 2 years need not be continuous. And you cannot have used the exclusion on another home in the last 2 years.
48|Two parts of home sale gain are always taxed. First, gain equal to depreciation claimed after May 6, 1997, for example for a rental period or home office, taxed at up to 25%. Second, gain for periods after 2008 when the home was not your main home before you moved in.
49|If you rent out your home for 14 days or fewer in the year, the rent is tax-free and you do not report it. You also deduct no rental expenses. Mortgage interest and property tax stay on Schedule A as usual.
50|A property used both for rent and personally is treated as a residence if you use it more than 14 days, or more than 10% of the days it was rented, whichever is greater. Then rental expenses cannot create a loss. Example: rented 200 days: more than 20 personal days triggers the limit.
51|Rental losses are normally passive. A real estate professional escapes that rule by spending more than 750 hours a year, and more than half of all working time, in real estate businesses. A full-time employee in another field almost never qualifies.
52|Passive losses you cannot use are not lost: they carry forward. When you sell the entire activity to an unrelated buyer in a taxable sale, all the stored losses are released and can offset any income.
53|Money that compensates for a physical injury or physical sickness is not income, including lost wages in that claim. Punitive damages, which punish the wrongdoer, and interest are always taxable. Damages for emotional distress alone, with no physical injury, are taxable.
54|An employer can give up to $50,000 of group life insurance tax-free. The cost of coverage above $50,000, from an IRS table, is added to the employee's wages on Form W-2, even though no cash was received.
55|An employer can pay for commuting tax-free up to a monthly limit: $325 for parking and another $325 for transit passes or vanpools in 2025. Amounts above the limit are wages.
56|A health flexible spending account lets an employee set aside pre-tax pay for medical costs: up to $3,300 in 2025. It is use-it-or-lose-it, but the plan may allow up to $660 to carry into the next year.
57|An employer plan can pay or reimburse child care tax-free up to $5,000 a year ($2,500 married filing separately) for 2025. The amount shows in box 10 of Form W-2 and reduces the expenses available for the dependent care credit.
58|An employer can pay up to $5,250 a year for an employee's education tax-free, whether or not it relates to the job. It can also be used to pay the employee's student loans. Amounts above $5,250 are wages unless another rule applies.
59|A minister's housing allowance is not subject to income tax, up to the fair rental value of the home plus utilities. But it is included when figuring self-employment tax. So it shows on Schedule SE and not as wages for income tax.
60|Five kinds of heirs are exempt from the 10-year rule and can spread payments over their life expectancy: the surviving spouse, the owner's minor child (until 21), a disabled person, a chronically ill person, and someone not more than 10 years younger than the owner, such as a sibling.
61|A health savings account goes with a high-deductible health plan. For 2025 you can put in $4,300 with self-only coverage or $8,550 with family coverage, plus $1,000 if 55 or older. Employer contributions count toward the limit.
62|To contribute to an HSA your health plan must have a high deductible: at least $1,650 for self-only or $3,300 for family coverage in 2025. You also cannot be on Medicare or be claimed as a dependent.
63|A 529 college savings plan can also pay tuition for kindergarten through 12th grade, but only up to $10,000 a year per student for 2025. The exam window tests 2025 law, so $20,000 is the wrong answer.
64|A Coverdell account is a small education savings account: at most $2,000 a year for each child from all contributors combined. Contributions are not deductible, but growth is tax-free when used for education, including grade school costs.
65|Interest on Series EE or I savings bonds can be tax-free if you cash them in a year you pay college tuition. The bond owner must have been at least 24 when the bond was issued, and income must be under the limits. Not allowed if married filing separately.
66|High earners pay an extra 3.8% on investment income such as interest, dividends, capital gains and rents. The tax applies to the smaller of the investment income or the amount of MAGI over the threshold. Example: single, MAGI $230,000, investment income $50,000: 3.8% × $30,000 = $1,140.
67|The 3.8% tax is only for investment income. It does not apply to pay from work, retirement account distributions or tax-exempt interest. But a retirement distribution can raise MAGI and push other investment income over the threshold.
68|Payment apps and card processors send Form 1099-K when a seller got more than $20,000 and had more than 200 transactions. The income is taxable whether or not a form arrives.
69|A U.S. citizen or resident who lives and works abroad can exclude up to $130,000 of foreign wages or self-employment income for 2025. Each spouse who qualifies gets their own exclusion. It does not cover pensions, interest or dividends.
70|Two conditions. Your tax home, meaning your main place of work, must be in a foreign country. And you must either be a real resident there for a full calendar year, or be physically outside the U.S. for 330 full days in a 12-month period.
71|Trading goods or services without money is still income. Each person reports the market value of what they received. Example: a painter paints a dentist's office in exchange for $800 of dental work: both report $800.
72|Income is taxable no matter how it was obtained. Money from theft, bribes or drug sales must be reported. A thief who returns the money in the same year has no income from it.
73|Jury pay is taxable as other income. Some employers keep paying wages and require the employee to hand over the jury pay. Then the employee still reports it, and deducts the same amount as an adjustment to income.
74|Payments from a state or licensed agency for caring for a foster child in your home reimburse the cost of care, so they are not income. Payments for more than 5 foster individuals over age 18 can be taxable.
75|Workers' compensation for a job injury or illness is fully tax-free. If the person goes back to light work, the wages for that work are taxable as usual.
76|A prize is income at its market value, even if it is a car or a trip and not cash. Example: win a $30,000 car on a game show: $30,000 of income. Narrow exception: certain awards given directly to charity.
180|Schedule B lists interest and dividends by payer. It is required when either total is over $1,500. It is also required at any amount if you had a foreign account or received interest on a loan you gave to the buyer of your home.
181|Tips are wages. An employee who gets $20 or more in cash tips in a month must report them to the employer by the 10th of the next month, so tax can be withheld. Tips not reported to the employer are still income, and the employee pays the Social Security and Medicare tax on Form 4137.
182|Large restaurants must assign extra tips to employees when reported tips look too low. These allocated tips are in box 8 of Form W-2, not in box 1. The employee adds them to income unless good records prove a smaller amount.
183|Dividends from real estate investment trusts are ordinary dividends, not qualified. But they get the 20% qualified business income deduction. Example: $1,000 of these dividends gives a $200 deduction.
184|When you sell your home and lend the buyer the money, the interest the buyer pays you is taxable. You list it on Schedule B with the buyer's name, address and Social Security number, because the buyer is deducting it.
185|If you buy a bond between payment dates, you pay the seller the interest earned so far. When you get the full interest payment, part of it is just your own money coming back. So you subtract that accrued interest from the interest you report.
186|A bond bought on the market for less than face value has a market discount. That discount is really extra interest. So when you sell or redeem the bond, the gain up to the discount earned is ordinary interest income, not capital gain.
187|If you pay more than face value for a bond, the premium lowers your real interest return. On a taxable bond you may choose to deduct a part each year against the interest. On a tax-exempt bond you must reduce basis each year, with no deduction.
188|The rule follows who paid for the policy. If the employer paid the premiums, or the employee paid with pre-tax pay, the benefits are taxable. If the employee paid with after-tax money, the benefits are tax-free.
189|Benefits from a long-term care policy are generally tax-free. A policy that pays a flat daily amount is tax-free up to $420 a day in 2025, or the actual cost of care if that is higher.
190|If you cancel a life insurance policy and take the cash value, you have income only to the extent the cash is more than the premiums you paid. That gain is ordinary income, not capital gain. Example: paid $20,000, receive $26,000: $6,000 ordinary income.
191|A life insurance policy can pay out before death when the insured is very sick. For a terminally ill person, expected to die within 24 months, the payment is tax-free. For a chronically ill person it is tax-free up to the cost of care or the daily limit.
192|An employer can pay up to $17,280 of an employee's adoption costs in 2025 without income tax. Social Security and Medicare tax still apply to it. The same expenses cannot also be used for the adoption credit.
193|A state Medicaid program may pay a person to care for a disabled individual who lives in the caregiver's home. These payments are treated like foster care payments and are not income. The caregiver may still choose to count them as earned income for the earned income credit.
194|Meals an employer provides at the workplace for the employer's own business reasons are not wages. Housing is tax-free only if the employee must live there to do the job, like a hotel manager who has to live at the hotel.
195|Under an accountable plan the employee shows the business purpose, gives receipts, and returns any extra advance. Then reimbursements are not wages and do not appear on Form W-2. If those rules are not met, the whole payment is wages.
196|A cash rebate on something you buy is just a lower price, not income. You reduce the basis of the item. Example: a $30,000 car with a $2,000 rebate has a basis of $28,000.
197|Income from an activity not run for profit must be reported as other income. But hobby expenses cannot be deducted at all for 2025. So a hobby is taxed on its gross income.
198|Alaska pays its residents a yearly dividend from oil revenue. It is taxable as other income. It often appears on a child's return and can trigger the kiddie tax.
199|Royalties from owning rights, like an oil lease or an inherited book copyright, go on Schedule E with no self-employment tax. A person who creates the work as a business, such as an active author, reports royalties on Schedule C and pays self-employment tax.
200|Payments based on need, such as Supplemental Security Income and welfare, are not income. Do not confuse SSI with regular Social Security retirement or disability benefits, which can be taxable.
201|Disability payments from the Department of Veterans Affairs are tax-free. A military pension based on years of service is taxable like any pension.
202|The part of a scholarship that pays for room and board is taxable. For a dependent student it is treated as earned income when figuring the standard deduction and whether a return is needed, which often removes the need to file.
203|Cryptocurrency is treated as property, not money. Selling it, trading it for another coin or spending it creates a capital gain or loss. Coins received as pay, or from mining or staking, are ordinary income at their value on the day received. Everyone must answer the digital asset question on Form 1040.
204|Starting with 2025 sales, crypto brokers report sales to the IRS on the new Form 1099-DA. For 2025 it shows only the sale proceeds. Cost basis is added for sales in 2026 and later, so for 2025 the taxpayer must supply the basis.
205|The wash sale rule blocks a loss when you buy back the same stock within 30 days. It is written for stock and securities. Crypto is property, so for 2025 you can sell at a loss and buy back right away and still claim the loss.
206|Under the Simplified Method you divide your after-tax cost in the pension by a number of expected monthly payments. The number depends on your age when payments start: the older you are, the fewer payments. Example: start at 62 with $26,000 of cost: $26,000 ÷ 260 = $100 of each payment is tax-free.
207|When a pension will continue to a survivor, the table uses the combined ages of both people when payments start. More expected payments means a smaller tax-free amount in each one. Example: ages 64 and 60 total 124, so 310 payments.
208|If you must give back Social Security benefits that you were taxed on in an earlier year, and the amount is over $3,000, you can take a deduction or a credit for the earlier tax, whichever helps more. Repayments of $3,000 or less give no deduction for 2025.
209|A back payment covering earlier years arrives in one year and can make more benefits taxable. The election lets you figure the taxable part as if each year's benefits had been received in its own year. You do not amend the old returns; all tax is still reported this year.
210|Form SSA-1099 shows total benefits before Medicare premiums are taken out. You start from that full amount when figuring taxable benefits. The premiums withheld count as a medical expense if you itemize.
211|Almost everything you own for personal use or investment is a capital asset: stocks, a home, a car. The law lists what is not: inventory, business equipment and buildings, money customers owe you, and art or writing in the hands of the person who created it.
212|The holding period starts the day after you acquire the asset. For a gift you add the giver's time to yours. Inherited property is always long-term, even if sold the day after the death.
213|The amount realized is everything you get from a sale: cash, the value of other property, and any of your debt the buyer takes over, minus selling costs like commissions. Gain or loss is this amount minus your adjusted basis.
214|Gain on your home is the selling price less selling costs, minus your basis (cost plus improvements). Up to $250,000 or $500,000 is excluded if you pass the tests. A loss on your personal home can never be deducted.
215|If your home office was a room inside the house, the whole gain can still be excluded. Only the depreciation you claimed for the office is taxed, at up to 25%. An office in a separate building is treated as a separate sale.
216|A day counts as personal use if the home is used by you or your family, by anyone paying less than a fair rent, or under a swap arrangement. Renting to a family member at a fair price as their main home is not personal use. Days spent mainly doing repairs do not count.
217|Costs are split between rental and personal days. If personal use is over the 14-day or 10% limit, rental deductions cannot be more than rental income. They are taken in a fixed order: mortgage interest and taxes first, then operating costs, and depreciation last.
218|A residential rental building is deducted evenly over 27.5 years. Land is never depreciated. In the first year you get a part-year amount based on the month it was placed in service. Basis goes down by depreciation you could have taken, even if you forgot to claim it.
219|With a nonqualified option you have wage income on the day you exercise: the stock's value minus the price you pay. It is on your Form W-2. Your basis in the shares is their value that day. Example: exercise at $10 when worth $30: $20 of wages per share, basis $30.
220|An employee plan lets you buy company stock at a discount with no tax at purchase. If you hold the shares 2 years from the offering and 1 year from purchase, only a small part of the profit is ordinary income (the discount) and the rest is long-term capital gain.
221|Restricted stock units are a promise of shares later. Nothing is taxed at grant. When they vest, the value of the shares is wages. That value becomes your basis, so selling right away gives almost no gain.
222|Every sale of stock or other capital asset is listed on Form 8949, short-term in Part I and long-term in Part II. The totals move to Schedule D, where gains and losses are netted and the tax rate is decided.
223|A gain can arise when insurance pays more than your basis in destroyed property. You can postpone the gain by buying replacement property within 2 years after the year of the gain. For a main home in a federal disaster area you get 4 years.
280|Box 12 uses letter codes for special items. C is the taxable cost of life insurance over $50,000. DD is the cost of health coverage and is only for information. W is employer money put in your HSA. Q is tax-free combat pay.
281|Each box has a different tax result. Box 1 is ordinary taxable interest. Box 3 is interest on U.S. Treasury debt, taxable federally but not by states. Box 8 is tax-exempt municipal interest. Box 2 is a penalty for cashing a certificate early, which you deduct as an adjustment.
282|Insolvent means you owe more than everything you own is worth. Cancelled debt is not income up to that shortfall. Example: debts $100,000, assets $70,000, so insolvent by $30,000. If $40,000 is cancelled, $30,000 is excluded and $10,000 is income.
283|With nonrecourse debt the lender can take only the property and cannot pursue you. So a foreclosure is treated as selling the property for the full amount of the debt. You have a gain or loss, and never cancellation of debt income.
284|Rent paid in advance is income in the year you receive it, for both cash and accrual landlords. Example: in December 2025 a tenant pays January 2026 rent: it is 2025 income.
285|A security deposit you must give back is not income. It becomes income in the year you keep it, for example to cover damage. A deposit meant to be the last month's rent is advance rent and is income when received.
286|If a tenant pays one of your bills or does work in place of rent, you have rental income equal to that amount. Example: the tenant pays a $300 plumber and deducts it from rent: you report the full rent and deduct the $300 repair.
287|Renting to a family member counts as real rental only if they pay a fair market rent and use it as their main home. If the rent is below market, every day is personal use, so you report the income but deductions are limited.
