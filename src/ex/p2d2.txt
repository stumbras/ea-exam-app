342|When a business is bought, part of the price is often for things you cannot touch: goodwill, customer lists, a promise not to compete, licenses. These are deducted evenly over 15 years (180 months), starting the month of purchase, no matter how long they really last.
343|Depletion is depreciation for natural resources like oil, gas, minerals and timber. Cost depletion spreads the basis over the units expected and deducts for units sold. Percentage depletion is a fixed percent of gross income from the property and can continue even after basis is used up. Timber must use cost depletion.
344|Many business credits are combined into one general business credit on Form 3800, with one overall limit based on tax liability. Unused credit goes back 1 year and forward 20.
345|A small employer that pays at least half of its workers' health premiums through the SHOP Marketplace can get a credit of up to 50% of the premiums it pays. It must have fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees with modest average wages, and can claim it for only 2 years in a row.
346|To encourage small businesses to start retirement plans, an employer with 100 or fewer employees gets a credit for the cost of setting up and running a new plan for its first 3 years, plus a credit for part of its contributions.
347|Meals an employer provides at the workplace for its own convenience, and snacks, are 50% deductible for 2025 and become nondeductible in 2026. Entertainment is never deductible. A party for all employees is fully deductible.
348|With the actual expense method you total the real costs of the car, including depreciation, and multiply by the business-use percentage. Example: $9,000 of costs and 60% business use gives $5,400. Driving between home and a regular workplace is commuting and never counts.
369|If a buyer stops paying on an installment sale of personal property and you take it back, you compare its market value with your remaining basis in the note plus repossession costs. The difference is gain or loss. Your basis in the returned property is its market value.
370|Taking back real estate you sold has a special rule so that you are not taxed on value you never got in cash. Gain is limited to the money you received before the repossession minus the gain you already reported. No loss is allowed.
371|Part of each dollar still owed to you is profit not yet taxed. Your basis in the note is the unpaid balance times the part that is not profit. Example: $60,000 still owed and a 40% gross profit percentage: basis is $36,000.
372|MACRS tables assume equipment was bought in the middle of the year, so the first year gets half a year of deduction. For 5-year property the first-year rate is 20% and the second 32%. For 7-year property it is 14.29%, then 24.49%.
373|Buildings are depreciated straight-line starting from the middle of the month placed in service. A building placed in service in January gets 11.5 months in year one: 3.485% for a residential rental (27.5 years) and 2.461% for a commercial building (39 years).
374|If more than 40% of the year's equipment is placed in service in the last 3 months, each asset is treated as placed in service in the middle of its quarter. A fourth-quarter asset gets only 1.5 months: 5% in the first year for 5-year property.
443|An accrual business reports income when it has earned the right to it and can measure the amount, even if not yet paid. It also cannot report income later for tax than it does on its audited financial statements.
444|Gross receipts are the income from the ordinary operations of the business: sales, service fees, and interest it charges customers on unpaid accounts. Gain from selling equipment is not part of gross receipts; it is reported separately.
445|If you deducted a debt as uncollectible and the customer later pays, the amount recovered is income in the year received, but only up to the deduction that actually reduced your tax.
446|A business can deduct a debt that becomes uncollectible, in whole or in part, as an ordinary loss. The amount must already have been reported as income. So a cash-method business cannot deduct unpaid invoices, since it never reported that income.
447|A personal loan that is not repaid, such as a loan to a friend, can be deducted only when it is completely worthless. It is treated as a short-term capital loss, subject to the $3,000 yearly limit.
448|A business that pays a non-employee $600 or more in a year for services must report it on Form 1099-NEC. This applies to payments made in 2025; the higher $2,000 threshold is for 2026 payments.
449|Payments to corporations usually need no Form 1099. Two exceptions are always reported even when the payee is a corporation: fees paid to attorneys and payments for medical or health care services.
450|Form 1099-MISC covers payments that are not for services by a contractor: rent of $600 or more, royalties of $10 or more, prizes, medical and health care payments, and the gross amount paid to an attorney in a settlement.
451|Form 1099-NEC must be given to the worker and filed with the IRS by January 31. The early date lets the IRS match income before paying refunds.
452|A business that files 10 or more information returns in a year, counting all types together (W-2, 1099 and others), must file them electronically.
453|If a payee does not provide a correct tax ID number, the payer must withhold 24% from payments and send it to the IRS. The payee claims it as tax paid on their return.
454|A business meal with a client or while traveling is 50% deductible if it is not extravagant and the owner or an employee is present. Entertainment such as game tickets is not deductible. Food at a party for all staff is 100% deductible.
455|Gifts to customers and business contacts are deductible only up to $25 per person per year. Small extra costs such as wrapping and engraving do not count toward the $25. Items under $4 with the company name on them are not treated as gifts.
456|In place of actual costs, a business can deduct 70 cents per business mile in 2025, plus parking and tolls. To use it you must choose it in the first year the car is used for business. It cannot be used by a fleet of 5 or more vehicles used at the same time.
457|Travel is deductible when business takes you away from your tax home long enough to need sleep. Transportation and lodging are fully deductible and meals 50%. A spouse's costs count only if the spouse is an employee with a real business reason to come.
458|A home office is deductible only if a specific area is used regularly and only for business, and it is the main place of business, or a place to meet clients, or a separate structure. Employees cannot claim it, even when working from home.
459|The simple way: deduct $5 per square foot of the office, up to 300 square feet, so at most $1,500. No depreciation is taken. The regular method uses actual costs times the business percentage on Form 8829.
460|Costs of investigating and starting a business, and of legally forming it, are capital costs. You may deduct up to $5,000 of each kind in the first year. That $5,000 shrinks dollar for dollar once the total passes $50,000. The rest is spread over 180 months.
461|Section 179 lets a business deduct the cost of equipment in the year it is placed in service. For 2025 the limit is $2,500,000. It is reduced dollar for dollar when purchases exceed $4,000,000, and it cannot be more than the business's taxable income; the extra carries forward.
462|Vehicles over 6,000 pounds escape the luxury car depreciation caps, so the law puts a separate limit on Section 179 for heavy SUVs: $31,300 for 2025. Bonus depreciation can still apply to the rest.
463|Bonus depreciation lets a business deduct the cost of qualifying property in the first year with no dollar limit. For property acquired after January 19, 2025 it is 100%, made permanent. Property acquired before that date and placed in service in 2025 gets 40%.
464|Five-year property covers things that wear out or go out of date quickly: cars, light trucks, computers, copiers and research equipment.
465|Seven-year property is the default class: office furniture and fixtures, farm machinery, and any property that has no class life assigned.
466|Fifteen-year property includes improvements to land, such as fences, roads, sidewalks and parking lots, and qualified improvement property inside commercial buildings.
467|Buildings are depreciated straight-line: 27.5 years for residential rental and 39 years for commercial property, with a mid-month convention. The land under the building is never depreciated, so the price must be divided between land and building.
468|Listed property, mainly vehicles, must be used more than 50% for business to get accelerated depreciation, Section 179 or bonus. If business use later falls to 50% or less, the extra depreciation taken is added back to income.
469|Buildings are depreciated straight-line, so there is no ordinary recapture for an individual. But the gain that comes from that depreciation is taxed at up to 25%, not the usual 15% or 20%. Gain above the original cost gets the normal capital gain rates.
470|C corporations have an extra rule for real property. 20% of the straight-line depreciation taken, or 20% of the gain if less, is treated as ordinary income. With a flat 21% rate it mainly affects capital loss use.
471|Section 1231 gives the best of both: add up gains and losses on business property held over a year. A net gain is long-term capital gain; a net loss is an ordinary loss. To prevent abuse, a net gain is ordinary to the extent of net Section 1231 losses in the past 5 years.
472|Form 4797 reports sales of property used in a business. It separates ordinary gain from depreciation recapture, Section 1231 gains and losses, and ordinary gains on property held one year or less.
473|You can swap business or investment real estate for other real estate and postpone the gain. You must identify the new property within 45 days and receive it within 180 days. Cash or other property received (boot) is taxed up to the gain. Equipment and vehicles no longer qualify.
474|When property is destroyed, stolen or condemned and the payment exceeds basis, the gain can be postponed by buying similar property in time: generally 2 years after the end of the year the gain was realized, 3 years for condemned business real estate, and 4 years for a main home in a federal disaster.
475|When payments arrive over more than one year, gain is reported as the money comes in, using the gross profit percentage. Depreciation recapture is the exception: all of it is taxed in the year of sale. Inventory and publicly traded stock cannot use this method.
476|A cost that only keeps property in normal working condition is a repair and is deducted now. A cost that makes the property better, restores it, or adapts it to a new use is an improvement and must be capitalized and depreciated.
477|A business may elect to deduct small purchases right away, not depreciate them: up to $2,500 per item or invoice, or $5,000 if it has audited financial statements. The election is made each year on the return.
478|Inventory is what the business holds to sell: goods bought for resale, raw materials, partly finished goods and finished products. Supplies that do not become part of the product are not inventory.
479|Inventory methods decide which costs are treated as sold. FIFO assumes the oldest goods are sold first; LIFO assumes the newest. LIFO needs an election on Form 970 and cannot be combined with lower of cost or market. Small businesses may treat inventory as supplies.
480|From 2022 through 2024, U.S. research costs had to be spread over 5 years. The new law lets them be deducted immediately again for tax years beginning after 2024. Research done outside the U.S. is still spread over 15 years.
481|Payroll tax has two parts, each paid by both the employee and the employer. Social Security is 6.2% each on wages up to $176,100 for 2025. Medicare is 1.45% each on all wages. The employee alone pays an extra 0.9% on wages over $200,000.
482|Federal unemployment tax is paid only by the employer: 6.0% on the first $7,000 of each worker's wages. Employers who pay state unemployment tax on time get a credit of up to 5.4%, so the usual federal cost is 0.6%, or $42 per employee.
483|Form 941 is filed each quarter, by the last day of the month after the quarter ends: April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31. Form 940 for unemployment tax is yearly, due January 31. Very small employers may be told to file the yearly Form 944.
484|Employers must give Form W-2 to each employee and file the forms with the Social Security Administration, with Form W-3, by January 31.
485|How often an employer deposits payroll taxes depends on its history. Look at total taxes in the 12 months ending June 30 of the prior year. $50,000 or less: deposit monthly, by the 15th of the next month. More than $50,000: deposit semiweekly.
486|If an employer builds up $100,000 or more of payroll tax on any single day, it must deposit by the next business day. From then on it is a semiweekly depositor for the rest of that year and the next.
487|A very small employer can skip deposits: if total payroll tax for the quarter is under $2,500, it can be paid with Form 941. Unemployment tax must be deposited once the amount owed exceeds $500.
488|Whether a worker is an employee depends on who controls the work. Three groups of facts: behavioral control (how the work is done), financial control (who pays expenses and takes the risk), and the relationship (contracts, benefits, permanence). Either side can ask the IRS to decide with Form SS-8.
489|A statutory employee is an independent contractor by normal rules whom the law treats as an employee for payroll tax. They get a Form W-2 with the box 13 statutory employee box checked, report income and expenses on Schedule C, and owe no self-employment tax.
490|Some workers are treated as self-employed by law: direct sellers and licensed real estate agents, when paid by sales and under a contract saying they are not employees. No taxes are withheld and they pay self-employment tax.
491|Certain benefits can be given to employees without tax: services the employer sells that cost it nothing extra (an empty airline seat), employee discounts, items needed for the job, small occasional items, commuting benefits up to $325 a month, and the first $50,000 of group life insurance.
492|An award of a physical item for long service or safety can be tax-free to the employee and deductible by the employer, up to $400, or $1,600 under a written plan. Cash, gift cards, vacations and meals do not qualify.
493|If an employer pays or reimburses an employee's moving costs, the amount is taxable wages. The only exception is for active-duty military moving under orders.
494|A SEP is a simple employer-funded plan. The employer can put in up to 25% of pay, with a $70,000 cap for 2025. For a self-employed owner it works out to about 20% of net earnings. It can be opened and funded as late as the extended due date of the return.
495|A SIMPLE IRA is for employers with 100 or fewer employees. Employees can defer up to $16,500 in 2025, plus $3,500 if 50 or older. The employer must either match up to 3% of pay or give 2% to everyone. A new plan must be set up by October 1.
496|For 2025 an employee can defer $23,500 into a 401(k), plus $7,500 at age 50 or older, or $11,250 at ages 60 to 63. Employee and employer contributions together cannot exceed $70,000, not counting catch-up amounts.
497|A defined benefit plan promises a pension amount, not an account balance. For 2025 the yearly pension that can be funded is limited to $280,000.
498|Retirement plans must not favor the highest paid. For testing, an employee is highly compensated if they earned more than $160,000 in the prior year, or own more than 5% of the business.
499|A retirement plan covering only the owner, or the owner and spouse, files Form 5500-EZ, and only when plan assets are over $250,000 at year end or it is the plan's final year.
500|Contributions for employees are a business expense on Schedule C. But the owner's own retirement contribution is not a business expense: it is deducted as an adjustment to income on Schedule 1, so it does not reduce self-employment tax.
501|A direct seller, such as someone selling products at home parties, is self-employed. Income and expenses go on Schedule C and net profit is subject to self-employment tax.
502|Depreciation on passenger cars is capped each year, however expensive the car. For a car placed in service in 2025 the first-year limit is $20,200 if bonus depreciation is used and $12,200 if not. Heavy vehicles over 6,000 pounds are not subject to these caps.
882|Form 4562 reports depreciation and amortization for property placed in service this year, the Section 179 election, and information on vehicles and other listed property.
883|A self-employed person uses Form 8829 to figure the home office deduction under the regular method. It splits home costs by the business percentage and limits the deduction to the income of the business.
884|Form 3800 combines the various business credits into one general business credit and applies the limit based on tax. It also tracks carrybacks and carryforwards.
885|Form 8941 figures the credit for small employers that pay for employee health insurance. The result goes to Form 3800.
886|Form 941 is the employer's quarterly payroll return. It reports wages paid, income tax withheld, and both halves of Social Security and Medicare tax.
887|Form 940 is the employer's yearly return for federal unemployment tax. Only the employer pays this tax; nothing is withheld from workers.
888|Form 944 replaces the four quarterly Forms 941 for the smallest employers, those with yearly payroll tax of $1,000 or less. The IRS must notify the employer to use it.
889|Form 943 is the yearly payroll return for employers of farm workers, used in place of Form 941.
890|Form W-3 is the cover sheet that totals all the Forms W-2 an employer sends to the Social Security Administration.
891|Form 1096 is the cover sheet sent with paper Forms 1099 and similar information returns. It is not used when filing electronically.
892|When it is unclear whether a worker is an employee or a contractor, the business or the worker can file Form SS-8 and the IRS will decide.
893|A business that receives more than $10,000 in cash in one transaction, or in related transactions, must report it on Form 8300 within 15 days. The rule exists to detect money laundering.
894|A business files Form 970 with its return for the first year it wants to use LIFO to value inventory.
895|When a business is sold, buyer and seller both file Form 8594 showing how the price was divided among 7 classes of assets. Whatever is left after the other classes is goodwill.
923|MACRS is the system used to depreciate most business property for tax. Each asset is put in a class (5-year, 7-year and so on) with a set method and convention, so the deduction comes from tables, not from estimates of useful life.
924|The General Depreciation System is the normal one, with shorter lives and faster methods. The Alternative Depreciation System uses straight-line over longer lives. It is required for some property, such as listed property used 50% or less for business, and can be elected.
925|The uniform capitalization rules require producers, and resellers above the gross receipts test, to add a share of indirect costs such as storage, purchasing and handling to inventory. Those costs are deducted when the goods are sold, not when paid.
926|Cost of goods sold is the cost of the items actually sold in the year. Formula: beginning inventory, plus purchases and production costs, minus ending inventory. It is subtracted from sales to get gross profit.
927|FIFO treats the first items bought as the first sold, so ending inventory holds the newest costs. LIFO treats the last items bought as the first sold. When prices rise, LIFO gives higher cost of goods sold and lower taxable income.
928|Qualified improvement property is an improvement to the inside of a commercial building made after the building was first placed in service. It is 15-year property and qualifies for bonus depreciation. Enlargements, elevators and structural framework are excluded.
929|Listed property is property that is easily used for personal purposes too, mainly cars and trucks. It needs detailed records of use and must be used more than 50% for business to get accelerated depreciation, Section 179 or bonus.
930|Depreciation lowered ordinary income in past years. When the asset is sold at a gain, depreciation recapture taxes that part of the gain as ordinary income. It is full for equipment (Section 1245) and limited for buildings.
931|Amortization is the same idea as depreciation, for intangible assets. The cost is deducted in equal amounts over a fixed period: 15 years for purchased goodwill and similar intangibles.
932|Bonus depreciation is an extra deduction in the year property is placed in service, taken before regular depreciation. It has no dollar limit and can create a loss. It is 100% for qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025.
933|The Electronic Federal Tax Payment System is the Treasury's free system for paying federal taxes online or by phone. Employers must make payroll tax deposits electronically.
934|Under an accountable plan, employees must show a business purpose, document the expense, and return any excess advance. Reimbursements made under these rules are not wages and are not reported on Form W-2.
935|A statutory employee is one of four kinds of workers, such as certain drivers and full-time life insurance salespeople, treated as employees for Social Security and Medicare. They still deduct their business expenses on Schedule C.
936|A business expense is deductible if it is ordinary, meaning common and accepted in that kind of business, and necessary, meaning helpful and appropriate. It does not have to be indispensable.
937|A capital expenditure buys or improves something that will last beyond the year, such as equipment or a building. It is not deducted at once: the cost is recovered over time through depreciation, unless Section 179 or bonus depreciation applies.
