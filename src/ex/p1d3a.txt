77|Teachers often buy classroom supplies with their own money. A K-12 teacher, aide, counselor or principal who works at least 900 hours in the school year can deduct up to $300 without itemizing. Two married teachers get up to $300 each, not $600 for one.
78|Interest paid on a loan for college can be deducted up to $2,500 a year without itemizing. The deduction shrinks as income rises and is gone above $100,000 single or $200,000 joint. Not allowed if married filing separately or if someone can claim you as a dependent.
79|A self-employed person gets three main deductions before AGI: half of the self-employment tax (like the half an employer would pay), health insurance premiums, and contributions to their own retirement plan. They reduce income tax but not self-employment tax.
80|Moving costs are no longer deductible for most people. The only ones who can deduct them are active-duty military members who move because of a permanent change of station order. Claimed on Form 3903.
81|If you cash a certificate of deposit early, the bank charges a penalty. You report the full interest and deduct the penalty as an adjustment to income, so you do not need to itemize. The penalty is in box 2 of Form 1099-INT.
82|The four new deductions (tips, overtime, car loan interest, seniors) go on the new Schedule 1-A. They come after AGI, so they do not lower AGI, but you get them whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. A married person must file a joint return to claim the tips, overtime and senior deductions. The car loan interest deduction has no joint-return requirement.
83|An extra $6,000 deduction for each person who is 65 or older by the end of the year, on top of the standard deduction. It shrinks by 6 cents for each dollar of MAGI above $75,000 single or $150,000 joint. Example: single, MAGI $95,000: reduction 6% × $20,000 = $1,200, deduction $4,800.
84|Workers in jobs that normally get tips, such as servers and barbers, can deduct up to $25,000 of tips from income tax. The tips must be voluntary, not a required service charge. The deduction shrinks above $150,000 of MAGI. Social Security and Medicare tax still apply to the tips.
85|Only the extra half of time-and-a-half is deductible, not all overtime pay. Example: regular pay $20 an hour, overtime $30: the deductible part is $10 an hour. The limit is $12,500, or $25,000 on a joint return. The overtime must be required by federal labor law.
86|Interest on a loan to buy a new car for personal use can be deducted up to $10,000 a year. The car must be new, finished in a U.S. factory, and bought with a loan made after 2024. Used cars and leases do not count. It shrinks above $100,000 of MAGI ($200,000 joint).
87|Medical costs are deductible only above 7.5% of AGI, and only if you itemize. Example: AGI $60,000, medical costs $7,000. The floor is $4,500, so $2,500 is deductible.
88|In place of actual car costs you can use a flat rate per mile. For 2025: 70 cents for business, 21 cents for medical trips and military moves, and 14 cents for charity work. The charity rate is set by law and does not change.
89|You deduct either state income tax or state sales tax, whichever is larger, never both. Sales tax helps people in states with no income tax. Property tax is added to either one, and the total is subject to the SALT cap.
90|Mortgage interest is deductible on up to $750,000 of debt used to buy, build or improve your home. Loans taken before December 16, 2017 keep the older $1,000,000 limit. Example: a 2022 mortgage of $1,000,000: only three-quarters of the interest is deductible.
91|What the money was used for decides the deduction, not what the loan is called. A home equity loan used to add a room is deductible. The same loan used to pay credit cards or buy a car is not.
92|Points paid to get a mortgage when buying your main home can be deducted in full that year. Points paid to refinance are spread over the life of the new loan. Example: $3,000 of refinance points on a 30-year loan gives $100 a year.
93|Premiums for private mortgage insurance are not deductible on a 2025 return. The law brings the deduction back for 2026. The exam tests 2025, so the answer here is not deductible.
94|Interest on money borrowed to buy investments, such as a margin loan, is deductible only up to your net investment income for the year. Any extra carries forward with no time limit. Example: $5,000 of interest and $3,000 of investment income: deduct $3,000, carry $2,000.
95|Cash gifts to public charities such as churches and schools can be deducted up to 60% of AGI. Anything above that carries forward for up to 5 years.
96|If you give stock or other property held more than a year that has gone up in value, you deduct its full market value and never pay tax on the gain. In exchange the limit is lower: 30% of AGI. Example: stock bought for $2,000, now worth $10,000: deduct $10,000.
97|The larger the gift, the more proof is needed. $250 or more: a written receipt from the charity. Property over $500: Form 8283. Property over $5,000: a qualified appraisal. If you get something back for a payment over $75, the charity must tell you how much is deductible.
98|You cannot deduct the value of your time or services given to a charity. You can deduct what you actually spent while volunteering, such as supplies and uniforms, and 14 cents a mile for driving.
99|Loss of personal property from fire, storm or theft is deductible only if it happened in an area the President declared a federal disaster. Then subtract $100 from each event, and deduct only the total above 10% of AGI. Example: loss $20,000, AGI $80,000: $20,000 − $100 − $8,000 = $11,900.
100|For certain major disasters named by Congress the rules are easier. The floor per event is $500, there is no 10% of AGI reduction, and people who do not itemize can add the loss to their standard deduction.
101|Before 2018 employees could deduct job expenses, tax preparation fees and investment fees above 2% of AGI. These deductions were suspended and are now gone permanently. An employee with unreimbursed work costs gets no deduction.
102|For 2025 the child tax credit is $2,200 for each qualifying child under 17. If the credit is more than your tax, up to $1,700 per child can be refunded as the Additional Child Tax Credit.
103|The credit shrinks by $50 for every $1,000, or part of $1,000, that MAGI is over $200,000 ($400,000 joint). Example: joint MAGI $410,500 is $10,500 over, which rounds up to 11 steps: the credit drops by $550.
104|New for 2025: the child needs a Social Security number that allows work, issued before the return is due. In addition, the taxpayer, or at least one spouse on a joint return, must also have a Social Security number. An ITIN is not enough for this credit.
105|A $500 credit for a dependent who does not qualify for the child tax credit: a child aged 17 or older, a dependent parent, or a child with an ITIN. It can reduce tax to zero but is never refunded.
106|The American Opportunity credit is for a student in the first 4 years of college, enrolled at least half-time in a degree program. It can be claimed for only 4 tax years per student, and not by a student with a felony drug conviction.
107|The Lifetime Learning credit is 20% of up to $10,000 of tuition, so at most $2,000 per return, not per student. There is no limit on years and no half-time rule, so it covers graduate school and single job-skill courses. It is not refundable.
108|Both education credits shrink between $80,000 and $90,000 of MAGI for a single filer, and between $160,000 and $180,000 on a joint return. Above the top number there is no credit.
109|A married person who files a separate return cannot claim either education credit. The couple must file jointly to get them.
110|For the American Opportunity credit, required books and supplies count even when bought at a regular bookstore. For the Lifetime Learning credit they count only if the school requires that they be paid to the school.
111|The earned income credit is a refundable credit for workers with low income. The maximum grows with the number of children, up to three: $649 with none, $4,328 with one, $7,152 with two, and $8,046 with three or more.
112|The earned income credit is for people who live on work income. If investment income such as interest, dividends, rent and capital gains is more than $11,950 for 2025, no credit is allowed.
113|A worker with no qualifying child can get a small credit only if they are at least 25 and under 65 at the end of the year, are not someone's dependent, and lived in the U.S. more than half the year.
114|If the IRS denied the credit in an earlier year (not for a math error), you must attach Form 8862 to claim it again. If the claim was reckless you are banned for 2 years; if it was fraud, for 10 years.
115|A married person filing separately can now get the earned income credit, but only if they have a qualifying child living with them more than half the year and they lived apart from their spouse for the last 6 months, or are legally separated.
116|The credit helps pay for care so you can work. For 2025 it is 20% to 35% of care costs, counting at most $3,000 for one person or $6,000 for two or more. So the largest credit is $1,050 or $2,100. It is not refundable for 2025.
117|The care must be for a child under 13 who is your dependent, or for a spouse or dependent who cannot care for themselves and lives with you more than half the year. The day a child turns 13, later costs stop counting.
118|Care costs cannot be more than what the lower-earning spouse earned, because the credit is for working. If one spouse is a full-time student or disabled, that spouse is treated as earning $250 a month with one child, or $500 with two or more.
119|Paying a relative for care can qualify, but not these: your spouse, the child's other parent, anyone you claim as a dependent, or your own child under 19. Paying a grandmother who is not your dependent qualifies.
120|The saver's credit rewards lower-income workers who put money into a retirement account. It is 50%, 20% or 10% of up to $2,000 of contributions per person. No credit above AGI of $39,500 single, $59,250 Head of Household, or $79,000 joint.
121|The Marketplace sends Form 1095-A showing premiums and the advance credit paid to the insurer. The taxpayer uses those numbers on Form 8962 to compare the advance with the credit really earned. Form 1095-A itself is kept, not attached.
122|Married people must file jointly to get the premium tax credit. The exception is a victim of domestic abuse or abandonment who cannot file with the spouse.
123|Adoption costs such as agency fees, court costs and travel earn a credit up to $17,280 per child for 2025. New for 2025, up to $5,000 of it is refundable. The credit starts to shrink at MAGI of $259,190.
124|A 30% credit for solar panels, solar water heaters, wind, geothermal and battery storage at your home. There is no dollar cap and unused credit carries forward. The law ends it for spending after December 31, 2025, so it still applies on 2025 returns.
125|A 30% credit for making a home more efficient: insulation, windows, doors and efficient heating. The yearly cap is $1,200, with a separate $2,000 for heat pumps. It ends for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.
126|The credit for electric vehicles is up to $7,500 for a new one and $4,000 for a used one. The new law ended it early: it does not apply to a vehicle acquired after September 30, 2025.
127|Small amounts of foreign tax, typically withheld on mutual fund dividends, can be claimed directly without Form 1116. The total must be $300 or less ($600 joint) and all of it must be on passive income shown on a Form 1099.
128|A small credit for people 65 or older, or under 65 and retired on total disability, whose income is very low. It is figured on Schedule R and is not refundable. Few people qualify because the income limits have never been raised.
129|Each employer withholds Social Security tax up to the wage base, $176,100 for 2025. With two jobs the total withheld can go over the yearly maximum. The extra is claimed as a refundable credit on the return. If one employer alone withheld too much, that employer must fix it.
224|A self-employed person can deduct health insurance premiums for themselves, their spouse, dependents and children under 27, without itemizing. Two limits: not more than the business's net profit, and no deduction for any month they could have joined an employer plan, including a spouse's.
225|Members of the National Guard or reserves who travel more than 100 miles from home for duty can deduct the unreimbursed travel costs as an adjustment to income. This is one of the few employee expense deductions still allowed.
226|Normally legal fees for a personal lawsuit are not deductible. An exception: fees in an unlawful discrimination or whistleblower case can be deducted before AGI, but not more than the award included in income that year.
227|HSA contributions you make yourself are deducted as an adjustment on Form 8889. Contributions taken from pay through an employer plan were never in your taxable wages, so they are not deducted again.
228|Alimony is deductible only under a divorce or separation agreement signed before 2019. The payer must give the recipient's Social Security number. For agreements from 2019 on there is no deduction and no income.
229|Premiums for long-term care insurance count as a medical expense, but only up to a limit that rises with age: from $480 at age 40 or under to $6,020 at 71 or older for 2025.
230|When you travel away from home mainly for medical care, lodging counts as a medical expense up to $50 per night for each person. A parent traveling with a sick child counts too, so $100 a night. Meals are not included.
231|A home improvement made for medical reasons is a medical expense only to the extent it does not raise the home's value. Example: an elevator costs $20,000 and raises value by $12,000: $8,000 counts. Ramps and widened doors usually add no value, so the full cost counts.
232|You can deduct medical costs you paid for yourself, your spouse and your dependents. You can also include someone who would be your dependent except that their income was too high or they filed a joint return, such as a parent you support.
233|Some payments feel like taxes but are not deductible: federal income tax, Social Security tax, homeowner association fees, and charges for local improvements like sidewalks. Transfer taxes on buying a home are added to basis. Property tax on foreign real estate is not deductible.
234|A state or local tax on personal property, such as a car, is deductible only if it is based on the item's value and charged every year. A flat registration fee or one based on weight is not deductible.
235|In the year a home is sold, the property tax is split by the number of days each party owned it. The seller deducts the tax up to the day before the sale and the buyer from the sale date, no matter who actually wrote the check.
236|Mortgage interest is deductible on your main home and one other home you choose. A boat or motor home can count as a home if it has a place to sleep, cook and a toilet.
237|Gifts are deductible only to qualified organizations. Money given directly to a needy person, to a political campaign or to most foreign charities is not. Raffle tickets, the value of your time, and donated blood are also not deductible.
238|Charities usually sell donated cars. If the claimed value is over $500, your deduction is the amount the charity sold it for, shown on Form 1098-C, not the book value. You may use market value if the charity keeps and uses the car.
239|Used clothing and household goods are deductible only if in good used condition or better. The amount is what a thrift shop would charge, usually far below the original price.
240|If selling the property would have produced ordinary income or short-term gain, the deduction is limited to your basis, not the market value. Examples: inventory, art given by the artist, and stock held one year or less.
241|These itemized deductions survived when the 2% deductions ended. The most tested are gambling losses up to winnings, the deduction for estate tax paid on income in respect of a decedent, and repayments over $3,000 under claim of right.
242|For 2025 there is no overall limit that cuts total itemized deductions for high earners. A new limit for taxpayers in the 37% bracket begins in 2026, which is outside this exam.
243|The 20% deduction is for business profit, not for investment income or pay for work. So capital gains, dividends, interest, wages as an employee, an S corporation owner's salary and a partner's guaranteed payments are all left out.
244|If your qualified businesses have a combined loss, there is no deduction this year, and the loss carries to next year, where it reduces that year's qualified business income.
245|Rental income gets the 20% deduction only if the rental is a real business. A safe harbor treats it as one if you or your workers spend at least 250 hours a year on rental services and keep separate books.
246|High earners cannot claim the new clean vehicle credit: MAGI must be at or below $300,000 joint, $225,000 Head of Household or $150,000 others. The price must be at or below $80,000 for vans, SUVs and pickups, or $55,000 for other cars.
247|A used electric vehicle bought from a dealer earns a credit of 30% of the price, up to $4,000. The price must be $25,000 or less and income limits are half those for new cars. Not allowed for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025.
248|The premium tax credit lowers the cost of insurance bought on the Marketplace. You must not be eligible for affordable employer coverage or a government plan such as Medicare, and cannot be a dependent. For 2025 there is still no upper income cutoff at 400% of the poverty line.
249|If the advance credit paid to the insurer was more than you were entitled to, you repay the difference on the return. For 2025, people with income under 400% of the poverty line repay only up to a capped amount.
250|For a child with special needs, as determined by a state, the full credit is allowed in the year the adoption becomes final, even if the parents spent little or nothing.
251|For a U.S. adoption, costs paid before the adoption is final are claimed one year later. Costs paid in the year it becomes final, or after, are claimed that year. For a foreign adoption nothing is claimed until it is final.
252|Some AMT comes only from timing, such as exercising incentive stock options. That AMT is not lost. It becomes a credit, figured on Form 8801, that reduces regular tax in later years when regular tax is higher than the tentative minimum tax.
253|You cannot claim a credit for costs that were paid with tax-free money. Subtract tax-free scholarships, Pell grants and employer tuition help from tuition first. The same expense also cannot be used for both a credit and a tax-free 529 withdrawal.
254|When a student is claimed as a dependent, only the parent can claim the education credit, and tuition the student paid counts as paid by the parent. If the parents could claim the student but do not, the student may claim the credit.
255|The credit percentage depends on AGI. It is 35% at AGI of $15,000 or less and falls by 1 point for each $2,000 until it reaches 20% above $43,000. Most taxpayers get 20%.
256|Care costs paid through a tax-free employer plan cannot also earn the credit. Subtract the excluded benefits from the $3,000 or $6,000 limit. Example: two children and $5,000 excluded: only $1,000 of expenses is left for the credit.
294|Qualified dividends and long-term gains are taxed at low rates, so they do not count as investment income for this deduction. You may elect to count them, but then they are taxed at ordinary rates. It is worth it only when the extra deduction saves more.
295|Investment interest covers only loans for taxable investments. Interest on a loan to buy tax-exempt bonds is never deductible. Interest tied to a passive activity follows the passive loss rules, and home mortgage interest has its own rules.
296|Add up personal casualty gains and losses for the year. Losses can offset gains even if not from a disaster. Only if losses are larger does the remaining net loss need to be from a federal disaster and exceed 10% of AGI.
297|If the seller pays the buyer's points, the buyer still gets to deduct them, as if the buyer had paid. In return the buyer lowers the basis of the home by that amount. For the seller they are a selling expense.
298|A gift counts in the year it is actually given. A check counts when mailed. A credit card gift counts when charged, even if the bill is paid next year. A promise to give counts only when paid.
299|An expense is medical if it treats or prevents a specific illness. A weight-loss program for diagnosed obesity, a stop-smoking program and a guide dog qualify. Things that are merely good for general health, like vitamins or a gym membership, do not.
300|You can deduct student loan interest only if you are legally required to repay the loan and it was for you, your spouse, or a person who was your dependent when you borrowed. A parent who voluntarily pays a child's loan gets no deduction.
301|For the earned income credit, earned income is pay for working: wages, tips and self-employment profit. It also includes employer disability benefits received before minimum retirement age, and combat pay if the person elects to count it.
302|Income that does not come from current work does not count for the earned income credit: pensions, unemployment, Social Security, alimony, child support and investment income. Pay earned by a prison inmate does not count either.
303|For the earned income credit the child must live with you in the United States for more than half the year. There is no support test here, unlike the dependency rules. Military duty abroad counts as living in the U.S.
304|Care counts if its main purpose is the person's well-being and protection while you work: day care, a nanny, before and after-school programs and day camp. Overnight camp, school tuition from kindergarten up, and care while you are not working do not count.
305|To claim the credit you must report who was paid: the provider's name, address and tax ID number on Form 2441. Form W-10 can be used to ask the provider. If the provider refuses, you can still claim the credit by showing you tried to get the information.
306|For the child tax credit the child must be under 17 at the end of the year, be your dependent, live with you more than half the year, be a U.S. citizen, national or resident, and have a Social Security number valid for work.
321|Unused amounts carry to later years for different lengths of time. Capital losses, investment interest and passive losses carry forward with no limit. Charitable gifts above the AGI limit carry forward only 5 years, so they can expire.
322|Credits you cannot use may move to other years. The foreign tax credit goes back 1 year and forward 10. The general business credit goes back 1 and forward 20. The AMT credit carries forward forever. Refundable credits never carry over because they are paid out.
331|Long-term care insurance premiums are a medical expense, but only up to a yearly limit based on your age. Anything paid above the limit is not deductible.
332|Not medical: cosmetic surgery done only to improve appearance, over-the-counter medicine (insulin is the exception), gym dues for general health, and funeral costs. Surgery to correct a deformity from birth, injury or disease does qualify.
333|Most employees cannot deduct job costs. Four groups still can, as an adjustment on Form 2106: reservists traveling over 100 miles, qualified performing artists, state or local officials paid by fees, and disabled employees with impairment-related work costs.
