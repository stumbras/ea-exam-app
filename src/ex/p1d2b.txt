288|Points are prepaid interest. On a rental property loan you cannot deduct them all in the year paid. You spread them evenly over the life of the loan. Example: $3,000 of points on a 30-year loan gives $100 a year.
289|A stock dividend gives you more shares, not money, so your share of the company is unchanged and nothing is taxed. It becomes taxable if shareholders could have chosen cash instead.
290|To encourage investment in small companies, gain on qualified small business stock can be excluded. The stock must be from a C corporation, bought at original issue, and held more than 5 years. Any part of the gain that is not excluded is taxed at up to 28%.
291|Medicare is not a high-deductible plan, so once you enroll in any part of Medicare you can no longer contribute to an HSA. For the year you enroll, the limit is prorated by month. Money already in the HSA can still be spent tax-free on medical costs.
292|Coverdell contributions stop when the child turns 18, and the account must be used or moved to a younger family member by age 30. Money left after 30 is paid out, with tax and a 10% extra tax on the earnings. The age limits do not apply to a special needs beneficiary.
293|Health insurance paid by an employer is the largest tax-free fringe benefit. The amount shown with code DD in box 12 of Form W-2 is only for information and is never added to income.
309|In a stock split you own more shares but paid nothing more. So you divide your original total basis by the new number of shares. Example: 100 shares bought for $6,000 split 2-for-1: 200 shares at $30 each. The new shares keep the original purchase date.
310|Dividends used to buy more shares of a fund are taxed the same as dividends paid in cash. Because you paid tax on them, they are added to your basis. Forgetting this means paying tax twice when you sell.
311|A return of capital is the company giving back part of what you invested. It is not income; it lowers your basis. After basis reaches zero, anything more is capital gain.
312|When you sell only some shares of a fund bought at different prices, you need a rule for which shares were sold. You can pick specific shares, use first-in first-out (the default), or for mutual funds use the average cost of all shares.
313|When a home becomes a rental, depreciation starts from the smaller of two numbers: your adjusted basis or the home's value on the day it became a rental. This stops a drop in value during personal use from becoming a deduction. Land is taken out first.
314|The rule is one-sided for personal things. If you sell your car, furniture or home at a loss, there is no deduction. If you sell personal property at a gain, the gain is taxable (a home may qualify for the exclusion).
315|If you sell property at a loss to a close relative or a company you control, you cannot deduct the loss. The loss is not wasted: if the buyer later sells at a gain, the buyer's gain is reduced by your disallowed loss. Related means siblings, spouse, ancestors and descendants; in-laws are not included.
316|When stock becomes completely worthless, the law treats it as sold for $0 on the last day of that year. That date decides whether the loss is long-term. Because the exact year can be hard to know, you get 7 years to claim a refund, not the usual 3.
317|When a gift's value was below the giver's basis and you sell at a loss, your basis is that lower value and your holding period starts on the gift date. You do not add the giver's time. When you use the giver's basis, you do add the giver's holding period.
318|Property received as pay is income at its market value, and that same value becomes your basis. Example: a consultant is paid with stock worth $5,000: $5,000 of income, and a $5,000 basis in the stock.
319|A loss on stock is normally a capital loss, limited to $3,000 a year. Section 1244 lets the original investor in a small corporation treat the loss as ordinary: up to $50,000 a year, or $100,000 on a joint return. Any loss above that is a capital loss.
320|Basis in a home starts with the purchase price and closing costs like title fees. Add improvements that add value or extend life, such as a new roof. Subtract depreciation and insurance payments for casualty losses. Ordinary repairs like painting do not change basis.
325|Military pay earned in a combat zone is tax-free: all of it for enlisted members, and up to a limit for officers. Because it is not taxed, it is normally not earned income for the earned income credit, but the member can choose to count it if that gives a bigger credit.
326|Interest on Treasury bills, notes and bonds is taxed by the federal government but cannot be taxed by states. This is the mirror image of municipal bond interest.
327|Interest on state and local government bonds is free of federal income tax. Two catches: it still must be reported, and it counts when figuring how much Social Security is taxable. Interest on private activity bonds is added back for the AMT.
328|Savings bond interest builds up inside the bond. A cash-basis person can wait and report all of it when the bond is cashed or matures, or choose to report the increase each year. The choice applies to all their savings bonds.
329|Normally the HSA limit is prorated by months of coverage. Under the last-month rule, if you are covered on December 1 you may contribute the full year's amount. The condition: you must stay covered through the end of the next year, or the extra amount becomes income with a 10% extra tax.
330|An ABLE account is a savings account for a person whose disability began before age 26. Total contributions from everyone are limited to the gift tax annual exclusion, $19,000 for 2025. Growth is tax-free when spent on disability expenses.
689|Schedule B lists each payer of interest and dividends when either total is over $1,500. Part III asks about foreign accounts and trusts, which is where a taxpayer first tells the IRS that an FBAR may be required.
690|Schedule C reports the income and expenses of a business run by one person. The net profit goes to Form 1040 as income and to Schedule SE for self-employment tax.
691|Schedule D brings together all capital gains and losses from Form 8949, capital gain distributions and carryovers. It nets short-term against long-term and applies the $3,000 loss limit.
692|Schedule E reports income that passes to you from owning something: rental real estate and royalties in Part I, and Schedule K-1 income from partnerships, S corporations, estates and trusts in the other parts.
694|Schedule F is the farm version of Schedule C. Net farm profit is subject to self-employment tax, and farmers have special rules such as income averaging and a single estimated tax payment date.
700|Partnerships, S corporations, estates and trusts usually do not pay the tax themselves. They send each owner or beneficiary a Schedule K-1 showing that person's share, and the person reports it on their own return, whether or not any cash was paid out.
701|Form W-2 is the yearly statement from an employer. Box 1 is taxable wages, box 2 is federal tax withheld, boxes 3 to 6 are Social Security and Medicare, and box 12 has coded items like retirement contributions. Due to employees by January 31.
702|Form W-2G reports gambling winnings above certain amounts and any tax withheld. All winnings are taxable even when no form is issued.
705|Form SSA-1099 shows Social Security benefits for the year. Box 5 is net benefits (paid minus repaid), the number used to figure the taxable part. It also shows Medicare premiums and any tax withheld.
706|Form 1099-INT reports interest of $10 or more from a bank or other payer, with separate boxes for Treasury interest and tax-exempt interest.
707|Form 1099-DIV reports dividends. Box 1a is total ordinary dividends, box 1b is the part that is qualified (lower rate), and box 2a is capital gain distributions. Box 1b is included in box 1a, not added to it.
708|Form 1099-B reports sales of stocks and other securities by a broker: the proceeds, the date, and usually the cost basis. The amounts go onto Form 8949.
709|Form 1099-R reports money paid out of pensions, annuities and IRAs. Box 1 is the gross amount, box 2a the taxable amount, and box 7 the code that tells why it was paid.
710|Form 1099-G reports payments from a government: mainly unemployment benefits (taxable) and state income tax refunds (taxable only if you got a benefit from deducting the tax).
711|Form 1099-NEC reports $600 or more paid to a person who is not an employee for services. The worker reports it on Schedule C and pays self-employment tax. Due January 31.
712|Form 1099-MISC reports other payments of $600 or more such as rent, prizes and awards, and royalties of $10 or more. Payments for services by a contractor moved to Form 1099-NEC.
713|Form 1099-K reports payments received through credit cards and payment apps. It shows gross amounts, before fees and refunds, so the seller must subtract those to find real income.
714|Form 1099-S reports the gross proceeds from a real estate sale. It may not be issued for a main home when the seller certifies the gain is fully excluded.
715|Form 1099-SA reports money taken out of an HSA. The taxpayer then shows on Form 8889 how much was used for medical costs, which is tax-free.
716|Form 1099-Q reports distributions from a 529 plan or Coverdell account, split into earnings and basis. Earnings are tax-free if the total was used for qualified education costs.
717|A lender sends Form 1099-C when it forgives $600 or more of debt. The amount is income unless an exclusion applies, such as bankruptcy or insolvency, which is claimed on Form 982.
718|Form 1099-OID reports the part of a bond's discount that counts as interest for the year. You report it as interest even though you received no cash.
719|Form 1099-LTC reports payments from a long-term care policy or early payments from a life insurance policy. They are usually tax-free within the daily limit; Form 8853 is used to figure any taxable part.
735|Form 2555 is used to claim the foreign earned income exclusion and the foreign housing exclusion. It shows which test you meet: bona fide residence or 330 days of physical presence.
736|Form 8949 lists every sale of a capital asset: description, dates, proceeds, basis and any adjustment such as a wash sale. The totals carry to Schedule D.
737|Form 4797 reports sales of business property, such as equipment or a rental building. It separates the part of the gain that is ordinary because of past depreciation from the part treated as Section 1231 gain.
738|Form 8824 reports a like-kind exchange of real estate held for business or investment. It figures the gain that is postponed and the basis of the new property. Since 2018 only real property qualifies.
739|Form 6252 reports a sale where at least one payment comes after the year of sale. Each year it applies the gross profit percentage to the payments received to find the gain to report.
743|Form 8889 is required for anyone with HSA activity. It figures the deduction for contributions, reports employer contributions, and shows whether withdrawals were for medical costs. Non-medical withdrawals are taxed plus 20%.
744|Form 8582 figures how much passive loss can be deducted this year. It applies the rule that passive losses offset only passive income, plus the $25,000 allowance for rental real estate, and tracks the losses carried forward.
745|You can deduct a loss only up to the amount you could actually lose: your cash and property invested plus debts you are personally liable for. Form 6198 figures that limit. It is applied before the passive loss rules.
746|Form 982 is filed to exclude cancelled debt from income, for example because of bankruptcy or insolvency. The price of the exclusion: you must reduce tax benefits such as loss carryovers and the basis of your property.
747|Form 8815 figures how much savings bond interest can be excluded when the bonds were cashed to pay for college. The exclusion shrinks as income rises.
748|An employee who did not report all tips to the employer uses Form 4137 to figure the Social Security and Medicare tax on those tips and pay it with the return.
749|A worker who should have been an employee but was paid as a contractor uses Form 8919. The worker pays only the employee's half of Social Security and Medicare tax, not full self-employment tax.
808|Fair market value is the price a buyer and seller would agree on when neither is forced to act and both know the facts. It is used to value gifts, inheritances, donations and property received as pay.
809|When a lender forgives a debt, you got the benefit of money you never repay, so the forgiven amount is income. Exceptions include bankruptcy, insolvency and certain main home mortgage debt.
810|Mortgage debt used to buy, build or improve your main home. When this kind of debt was forgiven, for example in a short sale, the cancelled amount could be excluded from income under a special rule; the basis of the home is reduced.
811|An IRA is a personal retirement account you open yourself, not through an employer. A traditional IRA may give a deduction now and is taxed on withdrawal; a Roth IRA gives no deduction and is tax-free later.
812|Net unrealized appreciation is the growth in value of employer stock held inside a retirement plan. With a lump-sum distribution, only the plan's cost is taxed as ordinary income now, and the growth is taxed as long-term capital gain when the stock is sold.
813|A health savings account is a personal account for medical costs, available only with a high-deductible health plan. It has three tax benefits: contributions are deducted, growth is not taxed, and withdrawals for medical costs are tax-free.
814|A high-deductible health plan is insurance with a deductible of at least $1,650 for one person or $3,300 for a family in 2025. Having one, and no other general coverage, is the requirement for contributing to an HSA.
815|A flexible spending arrangement is an employer account funded with pre-tax pay, for health costs or dependent care. Unlike an HSA it belongs to the employer plan, and unused money is mostly lost at year end.
816|A Coverdell education savings account takes up to $2,000 a year per child. Contributions are not deductible; growth is tax-free when spent on education, including elementary and high school costs.
817|A qualified tuition program, better known as a 529 plan, is a state-sponsored education savings plan. There is no federal deduction for contributions, but growth is tax-free when used for qualified education expenses.
818|An incentive stock option is an employee option with special tax treatment. There is no regular tax at exercise, but the bargain element is added for the AMT. Holding the stock 2 years from grant and 1 year from exercise makes all the profit long-term capital gain.
819|A nonqualified stock option has no special treatment. On exercise, the difference between the stock's value and the price paid is wages, with withholding. Later growth is capital gain.
820|An employee stock purchase plan lets workers buy company stock, usually at a discount of up to 15%. There is no tax when the shares are bought; tax comes when they are sold, partly as ordinary income and partly as capital gain.
821|A restricted stock unit is a promise to deliver shares after a waiting period. There is no tax at grant. When it vests, the value of the shares is wages and becomes the basis.
822|Original issue discount arises when a bond is first sold for less than its face value. The discount is interest that you report a little each year as it builds up, even though it is paid only at maturity. Your basis rises by the amount reported.
823|The net investment income tax is an extra 3.8% on interest, dividends, capital gains, rents and other investment income for people with MAGI over $200,000 single or $250,000 joint. Figured on Form 8960.
824|The foreign earned income exclusion lets a citizen or resident working abroad leave up to $130,000 of foreign pay out of income for 2025. It is elected on Form 2555 and covers only pay for work, not investment income or pensions.
825|A passive activity loss comes from a business in which you do not materially participate, or from most rentals. It can be deducted only against passive income; the unused part is carried forward until there is passive income or the activity is sold.
826|A net operating loss happens when business deductions are larger than all income for the year. It is carried forward to later years without a time limit, where it can offset up to 80% of taxable income.
845|Basis is the amount you have invested in property for tax purposes. It usually starts as what you paid. When you sell, gain or loss is the amount you receive minus your basis, so a higher basis means less tax.
846|Adjusted basis is the original basis after changes during ownership. Improvements raise it. Depreciation, insurance payments for losses and returns of capital lower it. Gain or loss on a sale is measured from adjusted basis.
847|With a gift, the giver's basis carries over to the person who receives it, along with the giver's holding period. So the gain that built up while the giver owned the property is taxed when the receiver sells.
848|Property inherited from someone who died gets a new basis equal to its value at the date of death. Gain that built up during the owner's life is never taxed. It can also be a step down if the value fell.
849|A gain is realized when you sell or exchange property for more than your basis. It is recognized when the law actually taxes it. Some realized gains are not recognized now, such as in a like-kind exchange or the excluded part of a home sale.
850|In a like-kind exchange, boot is anything you receive that is not like-kind property: cash, debt relief or other property. You are taxed on gain up to the amount of boot. Example: realized gain $50,000 and $10,000 cash received: $10,000 is taxed now.
852|A qualified dividend is an ordinary dividend from a U.S. or qualifying foreign company on stock you held long enough. It is taxed at the long-term capital gain rates of 0%, 15% or 20% in place of ordinary rates.
853|Material participation means you really work in the business, regularly and substantially. The best-known test is more than 500 hours in the year. If you materially participate, the activity is not passive and its losses are not limited by the passive loss rules.
854|Active participation is an easier test that applies only to rental real estate. You own at least 10% and make the real decisions, such as approving tenants and setting rent, even through a manager. It allows up to $25,000 of rental loss against other income.
855|A fringe benefit is pay in a form other than money, like a company car or health insurance. The rule is that it is taxable unless the law specifically excludes it, as it does for health coverage, small de minimis items and qualified retirement plans.
856|A person is insolvent when total debts are more than the market value of everything they own, counting retirement accounts. Debt cancelled while insolvent is excluded from income up to the amount of the insolvency.
857|With recourse debt the borrower is personally responsible, so the lender can pursue other assets if the collateral is not enough. With nonrecourse debt the lender can only take the collateral. The difference decides whether a foreclosure creates cancellation of debt income.
